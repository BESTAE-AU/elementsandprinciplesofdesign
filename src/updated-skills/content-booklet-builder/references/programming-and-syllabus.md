# Programming units and syllabus versions

Use when a booklet sits inside a unit of work or program, or when mapping outcomes.

**Authoritative syllabus and version → outcomes and content → evidence → sequence → strategies and resources → assessment → evaluation.**

## Terms

A syllabus specifies intended learning and course requirements. A program sets out planned teaching, learning, assessment and resources. A scope and sequence maps progression and timing across units. A lesson sequence connects instruction and practice within a unit. Learning intentions state the intended learning; success criteria describe what achieving it looks like for this task.

## Unit-writing sequence

1. Confirm course, stage, cohort and the governing syllabus edition.
2. Identify the exact outcomes and content, recorded separately from paraphrases.
3. Decide the culminating evidence and a realistic task.
4. Map conceptual and practical prerequisites.
5. Plan explicit instruction, inquiry, and guided then independent practice.
6. Build in formative checks, differentiation, literacy and numeracy, and approved safety procedures.
7. Allocate resources, timing and assessment checkpoints.
8. Check alignment, coverage, accessibility and feasibility.
9. Record what happened and the revisions for next time.

A program should be usable by another teacher without unstated assumptions. In practical units, explain the learning the product enables; the product alone doesn't define the unit. Distinguish an assessment opportunity from a formal task.

## Common mapping errors

- Copying every outcome into every lesson, which hides actual coverage.
- Listing "critical thinking" without naming the reasoning students do.
- Assuming a recycled-material project covers all sustainability content.
- Treating a cultural workshop as cross-curriculum coverage without subject-specific evidence.
- A generic "differentiate as needed" column detached from the activities.

Prefer explicit relationships: this content point is taught through this investigation, practised through this comparison and assessed through this justification or performance.

Don't treat publication year as implementation year, and don't reuse old outcome codes for a new syllabus. To compare versions, use a table: source version | exact content | changed emphasis | teaching implications | assessment implications. Avoid one-to-one equivalence unless it's demonstrated. These checks don't establish the status of other TAS courses.

## Cross-curricular planning

Start from a shared inquiry and name each subject's actual contribution. General capabilities and cross-curriculum priorities enrich subject requirements but don't replace them. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspectives need local relationships, appropriate resources and protocols, not thematic decoration (see `inclusive-design.md`).
