# Barriers, constructs and reviewing adjustments

A fallback reasoning aid when the Diverse Learning project sources don't cover a situation. The school's adjustment bank always comes first.

## Identify the construct before adjusting

Ask what the task actually assesses. If handwriting isn't the construct, another response mode removes a barrier. If independent machine operation is the construct, a written explanation is only partial evidence. Distinguish an access adjustment from changing the outcome, and flag the second rather than deciding it silently.

UDL plans options proactively for everyone; differentiation responds to readiness, interests and context; individual adjustments address a specific student's barrier. They overlap but aren't interchangeable.

## Barrier → response → what to preserve

| Barrier | Possible response | Preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Dense written directions | Short sequence, visual model, oral explanation | The required decisions |
| Limited writing fluency | Oral or annotated response where permitted | The subject reasoning |
| Executive-function load | Milestones, checklists, visible materials | Meaningful independence |
| Limited equipment at home | School resources, equivalent low-cost methods | The project goals |
| Sensory barriers | Captions, text alternatives, quieter setup | Essential information |
| Physical access | Adapted equipment or workspace, support | Safe participation |
| Advanced prior knowledge | Complex constraints, transfer tasks | Genuinely new learning |

Keep technical vocabulary while providing a route into it; removing every technical term denies access to the discipline.

## Equity checks for TAS tasks

Does the task measure the skill or the household's resources? A task that needs a professional camera may measure purchasing power more than videography. Check hidden costs, devices, transport, workspace, attendance and home support, and offer alternatives with a comparable chance to show the learning.

Example (camera task): one student needs a stable support because handheld operation is hard, another needs captioned instructions, another a staged shot list. All can still make purposeful framing and communication decisions. If a specific physical operation is assessed, decide equivalent evidence with authorised staff.

## Practical classes

Review reach, noise, lighting, visibility of demonstrations, supervision and safe equipment use with the relevant staff and the student. An alternative task should assess the intended learning, not unrelated busywork.

## Review questions

Did the student access the content and participate safely? Did they show the intended learning? Do they use the support independently? Which barriers remain, and did the adjustment create new ones? Include the student's own view, not only an adult's impression.

Don't assign a permanently easier pathway from a diagnosis, language background or past grade alone; review supports as things change. Don't infer trauma or a diagnosis from behaviour, and don't invent legal entitlements; formal decisions follow current policy.
