# Task audit, validity and item design

Extra checks to run on a drafted task. The main workflow is in `SKILL.md`.

## Quality terms

- **Validity:** the intended interpretation of results is supported by the evidence the task produces.
- **Reliability:** judgements are consistent across markers and occasions.
- **Equity:** fair opportunity to show learning, with access and context considered.
- **Authenticity:** meaningful application, not just a realistic-looking scenario.
- **Transparency:** clear evidence requirements, standards and submission conditions.

Match the evidence to the construct. A polished folio may be weak evidence of independent design reasoning; an oral explanation can show reasoning better when writing isn't the construct; a written description alone can't establish a practical procedure.

## Five-relationship audit (before issue)

1. The syllabus demands what the task asks.
2. Teaching has prepared students for that demand.
3. The deliverables make it observable.
4. The rubric criteria describe its quality.
5. Marks and weighting reflect its importance.

Then check the arithmetic, dates, submission locations, required quantities, conflicting instructions and resource assumptions. Make hidden expectations explicit. For example, if two platform versions of a video are required, say whether the same footage may be reused and what distinct evidence each version must show.

## Command words and demand

Identify = recognition; explain = reasons or relationships; analyse = parts and interactions; justify = supporting a decision; evaluate = judgement against criteria and evidence. Don't ask students to evaluate and then reward only description. In projects, separate concept, research, development, production and evaluation, and avoid double-counting one quality under several vague criteria.

## Diagnostic vs summative evidence

A diagnostic question can locate a misconception but can't support a final grade. A final artefact can show production quality without revealing research or independent decisions. Combine evidence sources according to the construct rather than collecting everything. Familiarise students with an unfamiliar response format without supplying the graded answer.

## Item design and access (quizzes, Wayground, Canvas quizzes)

Apply these guardrails at creation, check them before output, and fix them where possible without changing the construct:

- **Video:** always captions, a transcript or an equivalent text summary. Asynchronous video needs a transcript or summary.
- **Visual-dependent items:** always a text alternative or equivalent access pathway (for example a data table for a graph). Check that the alternative doesn't give away the answer; a summary that states the assessed conclusion changes the construct.
- **Read-aloud:** if read-aloud is on and a visual is unlabelled with no text alternative, don't publish.
- **Multiple-choice and multi-select stems:** at most 30 words unless reading is the construct.
- **Distractors:** plausible, parallel in structure and length, and no trick wording.

These guardrails don't by themselves prove WCAG conformance.

## Moderation and integrity

Use anchor samples, independent marking of a sample, discussion of evidence and documented resolutions. Teach attribution and define permitted assistance, including AI. An impression that work "looks AI-generated" is not proof; use process evidence and school procedures. Don't invent late-submission rules, outcomes or AI permissions; check the governing policy and syllabus version.
