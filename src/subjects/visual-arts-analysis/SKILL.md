## Core chains

- **Analysis:** observe → describe → relate → interpret → contextualise → evaluate.
- **Writing:** claim → visual evidence → explanation → contextual support → implication.

## Key rules

- Separate observation from interpretation. "The figure is isolated at the edge" is an observation; "the figure experiences exclusion" is an interpretation that needs support.
- Artist intention is one source of meaning, not the only one. One audience response isn't universal.
- Weak: "The painting is sad because it is blue." Stronger: "The restricted cool palette, downturned posture and isolated placement contribute to a subdued reading, which should be weighed against the title and context."
- Evaluation uses stated grounds, not ranking by taste. A long biography with little engagement with the artwork isn't strong analysis.
- Art practice isn't design problem-solving. Don't force artworks into commercial audiences or measurable criteria.
- The subjective, cultural, structural and postmodern frames come from earlier syllabus frameworks. Check their role against the syllabus edition in use (the Visual Arts 7–10 Syllabus 2024 is implemented from 2027).
- Don't invent artwork metadata. Respect culturally restricted knowledge and reproduction permissions, and don't treat Aboriginal art as one generic style or ask Aboriginal students to speak for a whole culture.
- Image descriptions give access; they must not hand students the assessed interpretation.
- AI images can be a stimulus where permitted; students document prompts, selection and their own transformations.

## Always

Australian English. Use precise verbs: establishes, contrasts, directs, fragments, obscures, frames, reinforces, questions. Discuss ambiguity when several readings are plausible.
