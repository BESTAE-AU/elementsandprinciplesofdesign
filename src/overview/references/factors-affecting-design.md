
# Factors Affecting Design

The Factors Affecting Design are the lenses TAS uses to judge whether a design decision is appropriate. This guide applies them to any element, principle, product or project. The shared method is in `method/justification.md` and `method/checks-and-language.md`.

**Key rule:** apply the factors that genuinely bear on the decision. Forcing all fifteen into every answer produces padding and hides the real issues.

## The factors

| Factor | Question to ask | Example (student-designed reusable lunch wrap) |
|---|---|---|
| Appropriateness | Does it suit the need, users and context? Would another solution suit better? | Does a wrap suit a student who carries sandwiches, or would a box be better? |
| Needs | What real need does it meet? Is that need evidenced or assumed? | Survey of 30 students: 18 use cling film daily. |
| Function | What must it do, and does it? | Seal, stay clean, fold small. |
| Aesthetics | What visual or sensory character does it have? Describe before interpreting. | Print scale, colour and feel of the fabric. |
| Finance / cost | Does it change time, materials, tooling or price? | Beeswax fabric costs more than cotton. |
| Ergonomics | Comfort, grip, reach, effort? | Can small hands fold it? |
| WHS / safety | Hazards in making, using, cleaning or disposing? Safety outranks style. | Food-safe wax; no loose threads. |
| Quality | Will it perform consistently to standard? | Wax cracking after washes. |
| Resources | Skills, equipment, materials and time needed? | Access to an iron and wax in class. |
| Environmental (short and long term) | Waste, energy and materials now and later? | Replaces daily plastic; wax re-coating. |
| Sustainability | Durability, repair, reuse, lower impact, without superficial claims? | Lasts about a year; compostable cotton. |
| Obsolescence | Will it date or stop working? | Wax wears; can be re-waxed. |
| Life cycle | Extraction → making → use → end of life. | Cotton growing impacts vs plastic. |
| Accessibility | Usable by people with different needs? | Clear fold instructions; easy grip tabs. |
| Ethics | Could it mislead, exclude, stereotype or appropriate? | Honest "plastic-free" claims only. |

## Workflow

1. **Identify the decision or design** and its brief.
2. **Pick the 3–6 factors** that genuinely matter, and say briefly why others are less relevant if asked.
3. **For each, ask the question,** gather evidence (research, testing, costing) and state the effect on the decision.
4. **Show the tensions.** Factors often conflict (cost vs sustainability, aesthetics vs function). Name the trade-off and how it was resolved (`method/justification.md`).
5. **Connect to elements and principles** where relevant, e.g. colour choice affecting ink cost, or texture affecting grip.
6. **Write it up** as justified decisions, not a checklist.

## Common weaknesses

- Listing all fifteen with one line each.
- "Minimal = sustainable" and other superficial sustainability claims (`method/checks-and-language.md`).
- Factors named with no evidence.
- No trade-offs between factors.
- Accessibility or WHS added as an afterthought.

## Teaching ideas

- **Factor sort:** give a product and 15 factor cards; students choose the top five and justify.
- **Tension pairs:** find two factors that pull against each other in a real product.
- **Before and after:** compare an original product and a redesign through three factors.
- **Life-cycle map:** map a simple product from extraction to disposal.
