
# Design feedback and marking

This guide gives feedback on, and marks, whole designs, where several elements and principles matter at once. For deep feedback on a single element, use that element's `<element>-element-of-design` skill (its feedback file). The shared method is in `method/feedback.md` and `method/justification.md`.

Assessment logic: **criteria → evidence → judgement → refinement.**

## Workflow

1. **Get the brief and criteria.** Find out the audience, purpose, outputs and the rubric or marking criteria. If there's no rubric, use `marking-rubric-builder` (if available) to generate NESA-aligned criteria first, and say they were generated. Without that skill, give feedback without a grade.
2. **Look at the whole design first.** Squint test: what reads first, second and third? Does that match the brief's priorities?
3. **Review the professional checklist** where relevant:
   - visual hierarchy
   - layout and spacing
   - clarity of message
   - consistency
   - readability and contrast
   - accessibility
   - fit for purpose
   - format- or platform-specific issues (bleed, safe areas, thumbnails, embroidery, screen sizes)
4. **Identify the elements and principles doing the most work,** positive and negative. For each, quote or point to the evidence and name the variable.
5. **Check the justification** if there is one, using the ladder in `design-justification-writing.md` or `method/justification.md`.
6. **Judge against the criteria.** Map evidence to each criterion and say which evidence supports the level.
7. **Prioritise.** Give one to three next steps, in order, phrased as tests or design decisions.

## Output format

- **Overall:** one or two sentences on what the design achieves and the biggest opportunity.
- **What's working:** specific, with evidence.
- **What's holding it back:** two to four points, each tied to a criterion and a visible part of the design.
- **Next steps:** one to three, in priority order.
- **Mark or level** (if requested): per criterion, with the evidence.
- **Teacher notes** (optional): a conferencing question, plus a short comment ready for reports.

Keep the tone warm, honest and specific. Specific praise ("the date block's isolation makes it easy to find") teaches more than generic praise.

## Common patterns in student work

- No clear first read.
- Too many typefaces or colours.
- Low-contrast text.
- Near-miss alignments.
- Even spacing everywhere, so nothing groups.
- A design that only works at one size.
- A justification based on preference.
- Accessibility not considered.

## Report-comment frames

- "___ has created ___, using ___ effectively to ___."
- "To improve, ___ should test ___ at ___ and refine ___."
