# Emphasis — principle of design

This skill covers **emphasis** as a principle of design. Principles describe relationships and organisational effects; elements (line, direction, shape, form, space, size and scale, time and duration, value, colour, texture, typography, layout and composition) are the variables that create them. So always explain *which elements* produce emphasis and *how*, and never treat an element and a principle as the same thing.

The shared method (analysis progression, justification levels, Factors Affecting Design, experimentation, teaching resources, feedback and marking, and language rules) is in the shared method files. For depth on a particular element, use its `<element>-element-of-design` skill. For whole-process help, use the method skills (`design-analysis-progression`, `design-justification-writing`, `design-feedback-and-marking`, and so on).

## What emphasis is

Emphasis is making one part of a design dominant so it's noticed first. It creates a focal point (and sometimes secondary accents) by making something different from its surroundings.

Keep it distinct:
- **Emphasis vs hierarchy:** emphasis is the focal point; hierarchy is the full ranked order of everything.
- **Emphasis vs contrast:** contrast is a main way to create emphasis, but isolation, placement and convergence also work.
- **Emphasis vs clutter:** emphasising everything emphasises nothing.

## Types and variations

- **Emphasis by contrast:** value, colour, size, shape or texture difference.
- **Emphasis by isolation:** one element surrounded by space.
- **Emphasis by placement:** at a strong point, the centre or the end of a path.
- **Emphasis by convergence:** lines, gazes or perspective pointing at it.
- **Emphasis by the unexpected:** one element breaking a pattern.
- **Emphasis in time:** a hold, a zoom, a sound cue.

## Levers: what a designer changes to create emphasis

- Contrast (any kind)
- Isolation in space
- Placement
- Converging lines and gaze
- Pattern-breaking
- Motion and timing

## Common misconceptions

| Misconception | Better understanding |
|---|---|
| "Making it bigger is the only way." | Isolation, contrast, placement and convergence often work better. |
| "You can emphasise several things equally." | Competing focal points cancel out; choose one and subordinate the rest. |
| "The focal point should always be central." | Off-centre focal points can be stronger in context. |

## Worked analysis (model for exemplars and annotations)

Scenario: A charity poster: a grid of 24 identical grey water bottles, with one bottle in the bottom-right filled with muddy brown water.

- **Identify:** "The design uses emphasis."
- **Describe:** "23 identical grey bottles in a regular grid; one bottle breaks the pattern through colour (brown) and content."
- **Explain:** "Because the grid is so regular, the single different bottle becomes the focal point immediately."
- **Analyse:** "Emphasis is created by breaking repetition and by colour contrast against a muted field. Its bottom-right position, at the end of a left-to-right reading path, also makes it the last thing met before the call to action beside it."
- **Evaluate:** "Effective for a message about unsafe water for a minority of people. However, the brown and grey are close in value; viewers with colour-vision differences may miss it. Darkening the muddy water would protect the emphasis."

Each level should add a link in the chain, not simply more words.

## Justification examples

- **Weak:** "I made it stand out because it's important."
- **Developing:** "I made the button red so people notice it."
- **Strong:** "I made the donate button the only saturated colour on the page and gave it space around it, so it's the clear focal point for supporters ready to act."
- **Sophisticated:** "In a five-second test, only 3 of 8 people noticed the donate button when it was one of three red elements. I made it the only saturated colour and added space around it; 8 of 8 noticed it. I muted the heading colour, trading some brand colour for a clearer action."

## Feedback patterns to look for in student work

- **Several competing focal points.**
- **Emphasis by size only.**
- **Focal point doesn't match the message's priority.**
- **Emphasis claimed without naming how it's created.**
- **Accessibility of the emphasis unchecked** (colour-only).

## Activity ideas for teaching resources

- **Find the odd one out:** design a grid where one element breaks the pattern.
- **Five ways to emphasise:** emphasise the same word five ways (size, colour, isolation, placement, convergence).
- **Focal point hunt:** mark the focal point in five ads and name its technique.
- **Emphasis in film:** find a scene where timing or camera creates emphasis.
