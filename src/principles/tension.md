# Tension — principle of design

This skill covers **tension** as a principle of design. Principles describe relationships and organisational effects; elements (line, direction, shape, form, space, size and scale, time and duration, value, colour, texture, typography, layout and composition) are the variables that create them. So always explain *which elements* produce tension and *how*, and never treat an element and a principle as the same thing.

The shared method (analysis progression, justification levels, Factors Affecting Design, experimentation, teaching resources, feedback and marking, and language rules) is in the shared method files. For depth on a particular element, use its `<element>-element-of-design` skill. For whole-process help, use the method skills (`design-analysis-progression`, `design-justification-writing`, `design-feedback-and-marking`, and so on).

## What tension is

Tension is a sense of visual or temporal stress created when elements seem unstable, about to move, crowded, cropped, nearly touching or pulling in opposite directions.

Keep it distinct:
- **Tension vs imbalance:** imbalance can be accidental; tension is used on purpose.
- **Tension vs contrast:** contrast is difference; tension is the energy created by unresolved relationships.
- **Visual tension vs suspense:** suspense is tension over time.

## Types and variations

- **Near-collision:** elements almost touching.
- **Tight cropping:** subjects cut off at the edge.
- **Unresolved diagonals and tilts.**
- **Crowding:** too little space around a subject.
- **Opposing directions.**
- **Temporal tension:** delay, withholding, sustained holds.

## Levers: what a designer changes to create tension

- Placement near edges
- Cropping
- Diagonals and tilt
- Reduced space
- Opposing vectors
- Timing and delay

## Common misconceptions

| Misconception | Better understanding |
|---|---|
| "Tension is always bad." | It's useful when the message is urgency, danger or excitement. |
| "Tension means clutter." | It's controlled relationships, not mess. |
| "Any diagonal creates tension." | Context decides the effect. |

## Worked analysis (model for exemplars and annotations)

Scenario: A thriller poster: the protagonist's face is cropped by the left edge, a knife blade enters from the right edge, and the gap between them is only 5 mm.

- **Identify:** "The design uses tension."
- **Describe:** "Face cropped at the left edge; blade entering from the right; a 5 mm gap between them."
- **Explain:** "The near-collision and cropping create unease; the eye is trapped in the narrow gap."
- **Analyse:** "Tension combines space (little of it), direction (opposing vectors) and cropping. Keeping the rest of the poster dark stops the tension becoming clutter."
- **Evaluate:** "Effective for a thriller audience. However, the 5 mm gap may disappear at thumbnail size, where the two elements seem to touch, changing the meaning. A wider gap in small versions would keep the effect."

Each level should add a link in the chain, not simply more words.

## Justification examples

- **Weak:** "I made it look tense."
- **Developing:** "I cropped the face so it feels uneasy."
- **Strong:** "I cropped the face at the left edge and brought the blade in from the right with only a small gap, so the near-collision creates unease that suits a thriller."
- **Sophisticated:** "At thumbnail size the gap vanished and the blade appeared to touch the face, which felt too graphic for the platform. I widened the gap for small versions, reducing tension slightly to keep it suitable."

## Feedback patterns to look for in student work

- **Accidental tension** (unintended cropping or near-touching).
- **Tension that undermines readability.**
- **Tension used where calm suits the brief.**
- **Tension claimed without naming the cause.**

## Activity ideas for teaching resources

- **Near-miss study:** move two shapes from far apart to touching and record the feeling at each step.
- **Crop for tension:** crop one photo calmly and tensely.
- **Suspense edit:** extend a hold before a reveal and compare.
- **Appropriate or not:** sort ads by whether their tension suits the message.
