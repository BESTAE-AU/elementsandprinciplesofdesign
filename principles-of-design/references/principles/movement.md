# Movement

## Definition

Movement is the actual or implied progression of attention, form, subject or information through space or time.

Movement may be:

- implied in static design
- actual in animation or film
- navigational in interfaces
- physical in products or spaces
- perceptual as the eye moves through a composition

## Creation

- directional line
- diagonal structure
- scale progression
- repetition
- rhythm
- blur
- cropping
- gesture
- perspective
- directional typography
- sequencing
- animation
- transitions
- camera movement
- editing
- scrolling
- interaction

## Movement vs Direction

Direction describes orientation or pathway.

Movement describes perceived or actual progression along or through a pathway.

## Relationships

- rhythm can produce movement
- direction guides movement
- contrast can redirect attention
- hierarchy can structure the order of movement
- asymmetry may increase movement
- tension may create dynamic movement

## Multimedia

Claude should distinguish:

- subject movement
- camera movement
- graphic movement
- eye movement
- transition movement
- temporal sequencing

## UI/UX

Movement may support:

- feedback
- state change
- spatial orientation
- progressive disclosure

Motion should not be decorative at the cost of comprehension or accessibility.

## Accessibility

Consider:

- reduced-motion preferences
- vestibular sensitivity
- flashing
- motion duration
- parallax
- uncontrolled autoplay

## Trade-offs

- energy ↔ distraction
- guidance ↔ manipulation
- animation clarity ↔ delay
- dynamic composition ↔ instability

## Misconception

**Misconception:** Movement means animation.  
**Correction:** Static compositions can strongly imply movement.
