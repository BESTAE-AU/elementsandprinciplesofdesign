# Contrast

## Definition

Contrast is the perceptible difference between elements or conditions.

Contrast allows components to be distinguished and can establish emphasis, hierarchy, separation, energy and clarity.

## Types

Contrast may occur through:

- value
- colour
- scale
- size
- shape
- form
- texture
- typography
- direction
- space
- density
- motion
- speed
- timing
- sound
- conceptual meaning

## Core logic

Contrast is relational.

A colour, size or typeface is not "contrasting" by itself. It contrasts **with another condition**.

## Relationships

- contrast can create emphasis
- contrast can strengthen hierarchy
- contrast can increase variety
- strong directional contrast can create tension
- excessive unrelated contrast can reduce unity

## Contrast and accessibility

Claude must distinguish **aesthetic contrast** from **functional contrast**.

Functional contrast can affect:

- text legibility
- focus states
- interface controls
- visual differentiation
- low-vision access
- colour vision accessibility

Colour difference alone may not provide sufficient luminance contrast.

## Time-based contrast

Contrast may occur between:

- stillness and movement
- slow and fast motion
- loud and quiet audio
- long and short duration
- dense and sparse sequences
- abrupt and gradual transitions

## Trade-offs

- differentiation ↔ fragmentation
- visibility ↔ visual aggression
- drama ↔ reduced harmony
- strong hierarchy ↔ weaker secondary content

## Misconception

**Misconception:** Contrast means complementary colours.  
**Correction:** Colour opposition is only one form of contrast.
