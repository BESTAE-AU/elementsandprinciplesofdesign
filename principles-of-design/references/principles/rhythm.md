# Rhythm

## Definition

Rhythm is the perceived beat, cadence or visual/temporal flow created by repetition, interval, sequence and variation.

## Types

- regular rhythm
- alternating rhythm
- progressive rhythm
- flowing rhythm
- random or irregular rhythm

## Core logic

Rhythm requires attention to:

- recurrence
- spacing
- interval
- timing
- progression
- variation
- sequence

## Elements

Rhythm may be created through repeated line, direction, shape, space, scale, colour, value, typography, motion, duration or audio.

## Rhythm vs Repetition

Repetition provides recurring material.

Rhythm concerns the **cadence or flow generated by that recurrence**.

## Multimedia

Rhythm is particularly important in:

- editing
- animation timing
- motion graphics
- music-image relationships
- title sequences
- pacing
- transitions

Visual rhythm may reinforce, complement or deliberately conflict with audio rhythm.

## Applications

Textiles: motif spacing and repeat cadence.  
Architecture: columns, openings, structural bays.  
Graphic design: repeated modules and spacing.  
UI: recurring content cadence.  
Film: shot duration and editing pattern.

## Trade-offs

- predictability ↔ monotony
- variation ↔ disrupted cadence
- rapid rhythm ↔ cognitive overload
- slow rhythm ↔ reduced energy

## Misconception

**Misconception:** Rhythm is simply repetition.  
**Correction:** Rhythm depends on recurrence plus interval, sequence or variation.
