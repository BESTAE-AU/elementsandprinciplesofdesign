# Harmony

## Definition

Harmony is the compatible and visually coherent relationship between different elements or components.

Harmony concerns how differences coexist without producing unnecessary conflict.

## Core perceptual logic

Harmony may arise through:

- related colour
- compatible proportions
- consistent stylistic language
- related materials
- controlled contrast
- visual resonance
- repeated characteristics

## Why designers use it

Harmony can:

- make complex compositions feel considered
- support aesthetic coherence
- connect varied components
- create visual comfort
- reinforce mood or brand character

## Creation strategies

- analogous or related colour relationships
- compatible typeface pairing
- recurring form language
- material compatibility
- related proportions
- controlled image treatment
- consistent stylistic conventions
- moderated contrast

## Elements

Harmony commonly involves colour, value, shape, form, texture, typography, space and composition. Time-based harmony may also involve compatible timing, sound and motion behaviour.

## Relationships

- harmony can support unity
- contrast can exist within harmony
- variety can enrich harmony
- excessive harmony may weaken emphasis
- tension may deliberately interrupt harmony

## Harmony vs Unity

Unity concerns whole-system coherence. Harmony concerns compatibility between parts.

## Applications

In branding, harmony connects multiple brand assets without requiring identical execution.

In multimedia, visual and audio components may harmonise through pacing, tone, rhythm or mood.

In textiles, colours, materials, surfaces and silhouette may relate harmoniously.

In interiors, material palettes, lighting, furniture and spatial proportions can be compatible without being identical.

## Accessibility

Harmonious low-contrast schemes must not compromise legibility. Compatibility is not more important than functional differentiation.

## Trade-offs

- visual comfort ↔ insufficient distinction
- compatibility ↔ sameness
- subtlety ↔ reduced hierarchy
- coherence ↔ lower dramatic impact

## Failure modes

- incompatible components may create unintended conflict
- excessive harmony may become visually undifferentiated
- deliberate disharmony may create tension, urgency or disruption

## Misconception

**Misconception:** Harmony means avoiding contrast.  
**Correction:** Contrast can occur within a harmonious system when differences remain purposefully related.
