# Variety

## Definition

Variety is the intentional introduction of difference within a design or system.

Variety can maintain interest, support differentiation, accommodate diverse content and prevent excessive uniformity.

## Creation

- colour variation
- scale variation
- typographic variation
- shape variation
- image variation
- texture variation
- layout variation
- motion variation
- rhythm variation
- material variation

## Core logic

Variety is most effective when differences remain appropriate to the system.

## Relationships

- variety can oppose or enrich repetition
- variety can reduce monotony
- variety can create contrast
- controlled variety can coexist with unity
- excessive variety may weaken hierarchy or harmony

## Applications

Brand systems often require **unity with controlled variety** so the identity can adapt across campaigns and touchpoints.

UI systems require enough variation to differentiate states and functions while preserving consistency.

Textiles may use controlled motif, colour and texture variation.

Product families may vary size, colour or function while retaining shared form language.

## Accessibility

Variation should not undermine predictability. Changes in appearance should communicate meaningful differences where possible.

## Trade-offs

- interest ↔ fragmentation
- flexibility ↔ inconsistency
- differentiation ↔ cognitive load
- expressiveness ↔ reduced recognition

## Failure modes

- too little variety: monotony or insufficient differentiation
- too much variety: loss of unity or clarity
- inconsistent variety: arbitrary visual language
- deliberate reduction: may communicate restraint or uniformity

## Misconception

**Misconception:** More variety makes a design more creative.  
**Correction:** Variety is useful when it serves purpose, communication and context.
