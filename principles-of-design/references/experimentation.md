# Design Development and Experimentation

## Design Development and Experimentation

Claude should encourage controlled experimentation.

Do not encourage students to make one design and simply claim that a Principle has been achieved.

Use:

| Variable Changed | Design Variation | Effect on Principle | Secondary Principle | Communication Effect | Suitability | Refinement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Encourage:

- thumbnails
- alternative layouts
- controlled iterations
- annotated experiments
- comparisons
- A/B testing
- mock-ups
- prototypes
- peer feedback
- user feedback
- decision matrices

Where practical, change **one significant variable at a time** to support cause-and-effect understanding.

---

## Principle-Specific Experimentation

### Unity
Create versions with low, moderate and high system consistency.

### Harmony
Compare related and deliberately conflicting combinations.

### Balance
Compare symmetrical, asymmetrical and intentionally unstable compositions.

### Alignment
Test grid-based, mixed and deliberately disrupted alignment.

### Proximity
Vary spacing between and within groups.

### Proportion
Alter relative component sizes while holding content constant.

### Hierarchy
Create versions with different priority orders.

### Emphasis
Change which element receives dominance without changing the content.

### Contrast
Test value, colour, scale, typographic, spatial and temporal contrast separately.

### Repetition
Compare exact, varied and progressive repetition.

### Pattern
Test regular, alternating, modular and disrupted patterns.

### Rhythm
Change interval, sequence and variation.

### Movement
Alter directional cues, progression and timing.

### Tension
Compare stable, moderately tense and strongly disrupted compositions.

### Variety
Compare low, controlled and high variation while monitoring unity.

## Branding Experimentation

Keep the same:

- content
- logo
- message
- core imagery

while manipulating the Principle.

Evaluate across multiple touchpoints.

Examples:

#### Balance
- symmetrical
- asymmetrical
- intentionally unstable

#### Hierarchy
- three different information-priority systems

#### Contrast
- value
- size
- typography
- colour
- shape

#### Unity
- increasing consistency across several touchpoints

#### Variety
- low, moderate and high variation within the same identity

#### Rhythm
- repeated graphic devices with different intervals

#### Tension
- controlled disruption within an otherwise stable brand system
