## Branding and Visual Identity

Claude must analyse Principles across the **whole identity system**, not only a logo.

Consider:

- logos and lock-ups
- typography
- colour systems
- grids
- spacing
- imagery
- iconography
- graphic devices
- packaging
- advertising
- social media
- websites
- merchandise
- signage
- environmental graphics
- campaigns
- motion identity

### Brand consistency

Ask:

- Which Principles remain consistent?
- Which may vary?
- Which Elements carry recognition?
- What rules ensure coherence?
- What degrees of variation are permitted?

### Brand recognition

Recognition may be strengthened by:

- repetition
- unity
- pattern
- recurring colour
- typographic systems
- consistent motion
- recurring shape
- stable spacing
- consistent hierarchy

Do not claim that visual consistency automatically produces strong brand recognition; context, exposure and distinctiveness also matter.

### Brand personality

Principles may influence perceived character.

For example:

- symmetrical balance may communicate stability in some contexts
- asymmetry may communicate dynamism in some contexts
- strong contrast may create bold differentiation
- restrained variety may create consistency
- tension may create energy or disruption

These are contextual interpretations, not universal meanings.

### Brand guidelines

Claude may recommend rules for:

- spacing
- clear space
- grid behaviour
- hierarchy
- repetition
- graphic-device placement
- contrast
- typographic scales
- image treatment
- motion
- responsive behaviour
- component consistency

### Cross-platform design

A Principle should be tested across:

- print
- mobile
- desktop
- social formats
- packaging
- signage
- merchandise
- video
- animation

A layout that works at poster scale may fail on a mobile screen. Principles must therefore be implemented responsively, not copied mechanically.

## Time-Based Design

Claude must distinguish:

**spatial organisation** from **temporal organisation**.

A Principle may change over time.

Examples:

- hierarchy may shift as text enters and exits
- emphasis may move between subjects
- rhythm depends on timing
- contrast may occur between successive scenes
- unity may depend on recurring transitions
- tension may build and release
- movement may direct attention sequentially

Analyse:

- sequence
- timing
- duration
- transitions
- pacing
- repetition
- interval
- changing hierarchy
- continuity
- anticipation
- entry and exit
- loops

## Digital / UI / UX Design

Claude should apply Principles to:

- websites
- apps
- dashboards
- forms
- navigation
- responsive layouts
- information systems
- interaction design

Consider:

- hierarchy
- grouping
- discoverability
- readability
- consistency
- cognitive load
- responsive reflow
- component systems
- state changes
- interaction feedback
- progressive disclosure
- accessibility
- user expectations

Important causal chains include:

**spacing + proximity + alignment → grouping → hierarchy → easier scanning**

**colour + value + typography → contrast → emphasis → clearer call-to-action recognition**

**repeated component structure → repetition + unity → predictable interface behaviour**

Claude must distinguish visual appearance from proven usability. Do not claim that a choice improves usability without explaining the mechanism or evidence.
