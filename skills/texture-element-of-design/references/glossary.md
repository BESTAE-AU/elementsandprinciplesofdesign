# Texture glossary

Format: **term**, plain definition, example, and *don't confuse with* where students commonly mix things up.

## Core terms

| Term | Definition | Example | Don't confuse with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Texture** | The surface quality of an object or image: how it feels, or looks like it would feel | Bark, silk, concrete, a grainy photo | Pattern, value |
| **Actual texture** (tactile, real, physical) | Texture you can really feel with your hand | Impasto paint, embossed business card, corduroy, sandpaper collage | Visual texture that only *looks* rough |
| **Visual texture** (implied) | The illusion of texture on a surface that is actually smooth or different | A photo of rust on a glossy poster; hatching in a pencil drawing | Actual texture |
| **Simulated texture** (realistic) | Visual texture that imitates a real surface as closely as possible | Photorealistic fur in a digital painting; wood-grain laminate | Invented texture |
| **Invented texture** (abstract, stylised) | Visual texture built from marks or motifs that doesn't copy a real surface, or exaggerates one | Van Gogh's swirling strokes in the sky; halftone dots in pop art | Pattern (although invented texture often uses repeated marks) |
| **Pattern** | A planned, repeated arrangement of a motif | Checks, stripes, a brand's repeating logo | Texture. Pattern repeats; texture describes the surface. Seen small and dense, a pattern *reads as* texture |
| **Surface quality** | The general character of a surface: rough/smooth, hard/soft, matte/glossy, wet/dry | "The glossy surface reflects the light" | |
| **Tactile** | Relating to touch; inviting touch | Soft-touch packaging | |
| **Haptic** | Relating to the whole sense of touch, including pressure and vibration; used in product and UX design | A phone's vibration when you press a button | Tactile (narrower) |

## How texture is seen

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Raking light** | Low light that skims across a surface from the side, casting small shadows that exaggerate texture | Photographing a canvas from the side to show brushstrokes; museums use it to study paintings |
| **Value** | Lightness or darkness. Visual texture is mostly built from small, rapid changes in value | Cross-hatching gets darker as lines get closer |
| **Specular highlight** | The bright spot where light reflects directly off a glossy surface. Sharp highlights say "smooth and shiny"; soft, spread highlights say "matte" | The glint on a glass bottle |
| **Scale of texture** | How big the texture's features are relative to the object and the viewer. The same surface changes texture with distance | Grass is texture up close and a flat green field from a plane |
| **Density** | How closely packed the marks or features are | Tight stippling reads darker and finer than loose stippling |
| **Directionality** | Whether the texture has a direction (wood grain, brushstrokes, brushed metal) or not (sand, noise) | Brushed aluminium looks different when turned 90° |

## Descriptive vocabulary bank

Group adjectives so students choose precisely rather than defaulting to "rough" and "smooth".

- **Roughness:** smooth, sleek, polished, even, uneven, rough, coarse, gritty, grainy, sandy, bumpy, lumpy, jagged, pitted, craggy, gnarled
- **Softness:** soft, velvety, furry, fluffy, fuzzy, downy, plush, spongy, springy, hard, rigid, brittle
- **Light:** matte, satin, gloss, glossy, shiny, lustrous, pearlescent, metallic, iridescent, dull, translucent, opaque
- **Wetness / stickiness:** slick, slimy, sticky, tacky, oily, waxy, dry, powdery, chalky, dusty
- **Structure:** woven, knitted, fibrous, stringy, ribbed, corrugated, quilted, crinkled, crumpled, cracked, crazed, crackled, flaking, peeling, layered, scaly, feathery
- **Age / wear:** weathered, worn, distressed, rusted, corroded, faded, scratched, eroded, patinated, pristine
- **Mood words it often creates:** warm, cosy, organic, natural, handmade, rugged, raw, industrial, clinical, luxurious, nostalgic, gritty, playful, calm, unsettling

## Mark-making techniques (2D visual texture)

| Technique | How | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| **Hatching** | Parallel lines; closer = darker | Directional, drawn, engraved |
| **Cross-hatching** | Layers of hatching at different angles | Denser shadow, fabric, mesh |
| **Contour hatching** | Lines follow the curve of the form | Rounded surfaces, wood grain |
| **Stippling** | Many small dots; denser = darker | Soft gradients, skin, sand, stone |
| **Scribbling / scumbling** | Loose, looping or tangled marks | Hair, foliage, energy |
| **Frottage** | Rubbing a pencil or crayon over paper laid on a textured surface. Developed as an art technique by Max Ernst (1925) | Found textures: leaves, coins, grates |
| **Grattage** | Scraping wet paint off a canvas laid over a textured surface (Ernst again) | Found texture in paint |
| **Sgraffito** | Scratching through a top layer to reveal the layer beneath | Fine light lines in dark areas, ceramics, oil pastel |
| **Impasto** | Paint applied thickly so brush or knife marks stand up from the surface (actual texture) | Van Gogh; expressive energy |
| **Dry brush** | A brush with little paint dragged across the surface so it catches only the high points | Wood, grass, weathered surfaces |
| **Sponging / dabbing** | Printing paint with a sponge, cloth or scrunched paper | Stone, foliage, clouds |
| **Salt / wax resist** | Salt on wet watercolour, or wax crayon under a wash | Crystalline or resisted textures |
| **Collage and assemblage** | Adding real materials to the surface | Actual texture; found objects (e.g. Rosalie Gascoigne's weathered road signs) |

## Texture in graphic design and typography

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Typographic colour** (typographic texture, text colour) | The overall grey tone and evenness of a block of text seen from a distance. Even spacing gives an even texture. Rivers, loose justification and mixed weights give a blotchy one |
| **Rivers** | Gaps that line up vertically through justified text, breaking its texture |
| **Grunge** | A 1990s style of distressed type and layered, textured imagery; David Carson's *Ray Gun* magazine (1992–95) is the usual example |
| **Skeuomorphism** | Digital design that imitates real materials (leather, felt, paper). The shift away from it is usually dated to Apple's iOS 7 (2013) |
| **Flat design** | Interface style with no simulated textures, shadows or gradients |
| **Background texture / overlay** | A subtle image (paper, grain, concrete) placed over or under a design to add warmth or depth |

## Digital texture

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Noise** | Random variation in pixel values. Added on purpose to add grit or reduce banding in gradients |
| **Grain** | Noise that imitates film grain; softer and more organic than plain noise |
| **Halftone** | Representing tone with dots of varying size, as in print. Used as a style (pop art, retro comics) |
| **Bitmap / dither** | Representing tone with black and white pixels only. Gives a lo-fi, printed or retro-computer look |
| **Blend mode** | How a layer combines with those below it. *Multiply* keeps darks (good for grunge on light backgrounds), *Screen* keeps lights (dust, scratches on dark backgrounds), *Overlay* and *Soft Light* add texture while keeping colour |
| **Clipping mask** | Limits a texture layer to the shape of the layer below, such as a texture only inside the letters of a heading |
| **Displacement map** | Uses one image's light and dark values to distort another, so type or graphics appear to wrap over a textured surface |
| **Seamless (tileable) texture** | A texture whose edges match so it can repeat without visible joins |
| **Texture map / normal map** (3D, games, Multimedia) | Images wrapped onto a 3D model. A *colour* map gives the look and a *normal* or *bump* map fakes surface detail in the lighting |

## Print finishes (actual texture on printed products)

| Finish | What it is | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Emboss** | Raises part of the paper with a die | Tactile, premium, draws the fingers |
| **Deboss** | Presses part of the paper down | Subtle, understated, crafted |
| **Blind emboss / deboss** | Emboss or deboss with no ink or foil | Minimal, luxurious |
| **Foil stamping** | Metallic or coloured foil pressed on with heat | Shine, prestige |
| **Spot UV** | Glossy clear coating on selected areas only | Gloss vs matte contrast you can feel and see |
| **Soft-touch (velvet) lamination** | A matte film with a soft, velvety feel | Premium, invites handling |
| **Uncoated / textured stock** | Papers with felt, linen, laid or recycled surfaces | Natural, handmade, eco |
| **Letterpress** | Relief printing that often bites into the paper | Tactile, heritage |

## Textiles

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Handle (hand)** | How a fabric feels: soft, crisp, stiff, silky, harsh. Affected by fibre, yarn, construction and finish |
| **Drape** | How a fabric hangs and falls (related to handle, but not texture itself) |
| **Weave** | How warp and weft interlace. *Plain* (flat, even), *twill* (diagonal ribs, as in denim), *satin* (long floats, smooth and lustrous) |
| **Knit** | Interlocking loops; stretchy, often with a visible rib or cable texture |
| **Pile** | Raised loops or cut fibres standing up from the base: velvet, corduroy, towelling |
| **Nap** | Fibres brushed in one direction, so the fabric looks and feels different each way |
| **Grain (fabric)** | The direction of the warp (lengthwise grain) and weft (crosswise grain) |
| **Surface decoration** | Adds texture: embroidery, appliqué, quilting, smocking, beading, shirring, felting, devoré |
| **Finishes** | Change the surface: brushing (softer), mercerising (lustre), calendering (smooth, shiny), stone-washing (worn) |

## Timber and materials

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Grain (timber)** | The direction and arrangement of the wood fibres |
| **Figure** | The visible pattern on a timber surface created by grain, growth rings, rays and how the board was cut (e.g. quarter-sawn oak's ray fleck) |
| **Open vs close grain** | Open-grained timbers have large visible pores (oak, ash); close-grained timbers have fine pores and a smoother surface (maple, cherry) |
| **Finishing** | Sanding through grits (e.g. 120 → 180 → 240) makes a surface smoother. Oil brings out the figure; a gloss finish adds reflection |
| **Knurling** (metals) | A pattern of ridges rolled into metal for grip |
| **Surface finish** (products) | Matte, gloss, textured (e.g. VDI or bead-blasted) plastics; affects grip, fingerprints and perceived quality |

## Quick-recall questions (with answers)

1. What is the difference between actual and visual texture? *Actual can be felt; visual only looks like it could be felt.*
2. A drawing of a pineapple uses carefully rendered cross-hatching to look like the real skin. Simulated or invented? *Simulated.*
3. Why does side lighting make texture more obvious? *It casts small shadows from every bump, increasing value contrast across the surface.*
4. Name two print finishes that add actual texture. *Emboss, deboss, spot UV, soft-touch, letterpress, foil.*
5. Why might a designer lower the opacity of a background texture? *So it doesn't reduce text legibility or compete with the focal point.*
6. What is "typographic colour"? *The overall grey tone and evenness of a text block's texture.*
