## Core chains

- **Fabric selection:** end use → required performance → candidate fabric → evidence or test → construction implications → justified selection.
- **Material reasoning:** property → performance → end use → appropriateness, with limitations and care consequences.
- **Construction:** design intent → fabric behaviour → construction method → equipment → sequence → sample → quality check.
- **Garment development:** inspiration → concept → wearer, audience and end use → aesthetic and functional criteria → alternatives → fit and material trials → construction → evaluation.

## Key distinctions

- Fibre alone doesn't decide suitability: cotton jersey and cotton canvas behave very differently.
- Stretch ≠ recovery; absorbency ≠ wicking ≠ water resistance; drape is hanging behaviour, not softness; fit is suitability, not tightness.
- Seam type, stitch type and seam finish are separate decisions.
- Elements of design in textiles can be **structural** (construction, fit, material structure), **decorative** (surface, visual communication) or **functional** (performance, comfort, use, safety). Say which.
- Classroom fabric trials give comparative evidence. Don't call them certified standard tests.
- Never invent universal machine settings (Baby Lock, Juki, heat press). Settings depend on the model, task, material and manual, so test on a sample first.
- Keep criteria distinct when assessing: appropriate design, creativity, and manufacture proficiency. A beautiful finish doesn't prove strong reasoning.

## Justification example

Weak: "I chose satin because it looks fancy." Stronger: "This satin's sheen reinforces the formal character under the intended lighting, and sample trials showed the drape needed. Its tendency to fray requires an enclosed finish." Refer to the actual fabric, not universal claims.

## Always

Australian English. Sustainability claims need evidence ("natural" isn't automatically sustainable; "recycled" isn't "recyclable"). Cultural references need authority and context. Keep WHS and supervision in view for machines and heat.
