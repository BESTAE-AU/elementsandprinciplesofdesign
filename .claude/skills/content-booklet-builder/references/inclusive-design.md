# Inclusive design

Genuine inclusion means adapting curriculum, pedagogy, assessment and environment — not just placing a diverse range of students into an unchanged classroom. Strong first teaching reduces (but never eliminates) the need for complex individual differentiation. Run every workbooklet through a barrier audit as part of drafting, not as a check tacked on afterward.

## Barrier audit — check for

Language load; assumed background knowledge; reading complexity; visual crowding; contrast; handwriting burden; response-space demands; cultural assumptions; prior opportunity to learn; technology requirements; vocabulary; sequencing; assessment mode. Ask "what barrier is this design creating?" before asking how to "fix" a student.

## Possible supports

Diagrams; vocabulary boxes; sentence stems; worked examples; oral rehearsal; multilingual resources; enlarged versions; accessible digital copies; speech-to-text; text-to-speech; flexible response modes.

## EAL/D learners

Identify the actual language demands of a task before designing it. Support through explicit vocabulary teaching, visuals, model sentences, oral rehearsal, sentence frames, clear instructions, multilingual resources where appropriate, and reduced *unnecessary* linguistic complexity — the scaffold changes, the disciplinary concept doesn't get reduced.

## Students with disability

Distinguish an **access adjustment** (how a student demonstrates the same learning) from **changing the intended learning outcome** (what they're expected to know or do) — these are not the same move, and only the first is a routine adjustment. In an Australian context this sits alongside consultation and reasonable-adjustment obligations, not optional generosity — flag to Britt where a adjustment decision looks like it might cross into changing the outcome, rather than deciding that silently.

## Gifted learners

Differentiate through depth, abstraction, complexity, pace, connection and agency — not simply more questions of the same type or type-C "extension" busywork.

## Equity

Don't quietly assume paid subscriptions, home printers, personal laptops, large amounts of homework time, or parental tutoring capacity when designing a task or its completion pathway.

## Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspectives

Include meaningfully rather than tokenistically where relevant to the topic: recognise Country, respect sovereignty, consider local context, use Indigenous-authored and community-supported sources, follow cultural protocols, avoid presenting Indigenous peoples as a single homogeneous group, and avoid restricting Indigenous perspectives to historical disadvantage or trauma alone — represent contemporary, diverse, strengths-based perspectives, not only historical ones. When something is genuinely about local or place-based cultural knowledge, never invent it — flag to Britt that it needs an Elder, Aboriginal Education Officer, local Land Council/AECG-equivalent, or other community-approved source, and don't generate that content unprompted.

Respect Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property (ICIP) and cultural protocols, including around restricted/sacred knowledge and references to deceased persons — flag rather than guess when a topic might touch either. Where useful, prompt Britt to a moment of critical self-reflection on her own material: whose knowledge is represented here, whose assumptions are being treated as the default, and does this reproduce a deficit framing without meaning to. Treat anti-racism as its own explicit check, not something folded into general kindness or inclusion — racism can operate through curriculum content and representation, not only interpersonal conduct.

## Do not

Differentiate by "learning style" (visual/auditory/kinaesthetic) — learner variability is real, VAK matching is not evidence-based (see `claims-to-handle-carefully.md`). Treat identical treatment as equivalent to equitable assessment.
