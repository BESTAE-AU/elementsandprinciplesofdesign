# Numeracy in TAS and Creative Arts resources

Use when a booklet involves measurement, scale, ratio, costing, data from testing or surveys, or financial decisions. Make the mathematics explicit; students don't automatically transfer it from Maths.

**Context → relevant information → representation → strategy → calculation → reasonableness → explanation.**

## Where it shows up

| Area | TAS application | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Measurement | Dimensions, fabric lengths, tolerances | Units, precision, tool resolution |
| Ratio | Aspect ratio, mixtures, scaled layouts | It's a relationship, not an amount |
| Scale | Drawings and models | Consistent linear scale and units |
| Area | Fabric, sheet stock, coverage | Layout and waste beyond nominal area |
| Volume | Capacity, material estimates | Cubic units, real geometry |
| Percentage | Waste, discounts, progress | Correct base |
| Costing | Quantity, unit price, contingency | Separate rates, totals and assumptions |
| Data | Test results, user responses | Sample, variation, missing data |

## Worked examples to adapt

- **Aspect ratio:** 1920 × 1080 reduces to 16:9 (divide both by 120); 1080 × 1920 is 9:16. Changing orientation changes composition, so ask what would be cropped or moved.
- **Scale:** a 240 mm panel in a 1:2 model is 480 mm full size. Doubling linear dimensions multiplies area by 4 and volume by 8, so calculate area from full-size dimensions.
- **Material allowance:** 2.4 m plus a 10% allowance = 2.64 m. The arithmetic doesn't show that 10% is right; fabric width, nap, defects and layout may need another estimate.
- **Costing:** quantity × unit price = material cost, then add identified waste and delivery. A cheaper price per metre isn't a cheaper project if usable width, waste or durability differ. Contingency is a planning allowance, not a measured cost.
- **Test data:** record units, conditions and repeats; keep raw observations alongside any summary; ask what the evidence can and can't establish about real use.

## Data and ethical reasoning

Who collected the data? Who is represented or missing? How was it measured? Does the conclusion follow? Mean and median answer different questions. A percentage without a sample size can mislead. Charts need honest scales and labels. Correlation isn't causation.

## Questions that build reasoning

How did you represent the problem? Which information was unnecessary? Why does that operation fit? Is the unit right? Can another method confirm it? Would doubling the length double the area?

## Inclusive numeracy

- Use concrete → representational → abstract forms as the concept needs: manipulatives, diagrams, worked examples, unit prompts.
- Separate mathematical understanding from English-language load, and unpack words with everyday and maths meanings (difference, product, table, volume, mean, power, factor).
- For anxious learners, use low-stakes practice, multiple strategies, and treat mistakes as information.
- Extend gifted learners through generalisation, open problems and comparing strategies, not more routine items.
- Don't diagnose dyscalculia or mental health conditions; note barriers and use school processes.

## Assessing numeracy within a TAS task

Credit interpretation, strategy, representation, accuracy, checking and explanation. A correct answer without units or reasoning is incomplete evidence, and an arithmetic slip can sit alongside a sound strategy. Map formally only against actual curriculum requirements (ACARA numeracy capability alongside the NSW syllabus).
