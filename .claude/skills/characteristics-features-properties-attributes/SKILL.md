# Characteristics, features, properties and attributes

Students often use these four terms interchangeably. This skill keeps them distinct and applies them to any design. The shared method is in `references/design-method.md` (§3 matters most).

## The four lenses

| Lens | Question it answers | Example: a café logo's steam lines | Example: a school website's menu |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Characteristics** | What can be observed or perceived? | Three curved lines with round ends | A horizontal bar of six text links at the top |
| **Features** | What does it let the design do? | Signals "hot drink" and links to the rounded wordmark | Lets users reach any section in one click |
| **Properties** | What measurable or technical qualities govern it? | 2 pt stroke; black, CMYK 0/0/0/100 | 16 px text; 44 px touch targets; 4.8:1 contrast |
| **Attributes** | How does it relate to audience, purpose, context and meaning? | May read as warm and relaxed for a neighbourhood café | Suits parents looking for dates quickly on a phone |

## Quick tests for sorting a statement

- Could you **point at it or see it**? → characteristic
- Does it describe **what it does or enables**? → feature
- Could you **measure or specify** it? → property
- Does it mention **people, purpose, context or meaning**? → attribute

Some statements mix lenses. Split them: "The thick 4 pt lines (property) make the logo readable on cups (feature), which suits takeaway customers (attribute)."

## Workflow

1. **List statements** about the design, or elicit them from students.
2. **Sort each into a lens** using the quick tests. Split mixed statements.
3. **Look for gaps.** Most weak analyses are all characteristics, with no features, properties or attributes.
4. **Connect across lenses** in the analysis progression (`design-analysis-progression`). Characteristic and property support explanation; feature and attribute support purpose and evaluation.

## Common mix-ups

- Features and attributes swapped. A feature is what it does; an attribute is why that matters to people.
- Properties described vaguely ("big") instead of measured ("48 px").
- Characteristics that are really interpretations ("friendly shapes"). Friendliness is an attribute; roundness is the characteristic.

## Teaching ideas

- **Card sort:** 20 statements about one product, sorted into four columns.
- **Split the sentence:** students break mixed sentences into lenses.
- **Four-lens annotation:** annotate one design in four colours, one per lens.
- **Gap spotting:** give a weak analysis and have students add the missing lenses.
