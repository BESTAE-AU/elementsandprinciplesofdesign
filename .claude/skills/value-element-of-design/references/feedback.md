# Value: feedback and marking

Use this file when a teacher shares student work or a justification and wants feedback, a mark or "what gets them to the top band" on value.

## Workflow

1. **Read the work against its brief.** Note the audience, purpose and every touchpoint or output named (cups, embroidery, a phone screen, signage…). Reproduction problems usually hide there.
2. **Start from what the student actually did.** Quote their words or point to the part of the design, and name the value variable involved (Variables in `SKILL.md`).
3. **Check for the patterns below** and the misconceptions in `knowledge.md`.
4. **Check production and accessibility.** Use the production table in `knowledge.md` and `method/checks-and-language.md`.
5. **Place the reasoning on the justification ladder** (weak → developing → strong → sophisticated; `method/justification.md`). Say which link in the chain is missing.
6. **If marking:** use the rubric supplied. If none is supplied, use `marking-rubric-builder` (if available) to generate NESA-aligned criteria first, mark against them and say they were generated. Without that skill, give feedback without a grade (`method/feedback.md`).
7. **Write it up:**
   - **What's working:** specific, not generic.
   - **What's holding it back:** 2–4 points, each quoting the student.
   - **Next steps:** one or two, phrased as tests or design decisions, in priority order.
   - **Model structure** (optional): a fill-in-the-blanks version of a top-level justification, with a note not to copy the numbers.
   - **Teacher notes:** current level, gap to the top, and a conferencing question.

Keep the tone warm, honest and specific.


## Feedback patterns to look for in student work

- **Hierarchy collapses in greyscale** — colours of the same value.
- **Low-contrast text** (grey on grey, pastel on white, text over busy photos).
- **Flat shading** — only one or two values, so form doesn't read.
- **Mood stated as fact** ("dark means sad") without context.
- **Value confused with saturation** in written analysis.
- **Dark print areas** with no allowance for dot gain.

## Justification examples (Year 9 poster)

- **Weak:** "I used a dark background because it looks dramatic."
- **Developing:** "I used a dark background with white text so the words stand out."
- **Strong:** "I used a near-black background with white headline text, giving very high value contrast, so the headline is readable from across the canteen. That suits a noticeboard poster that students pass quickly."
- **Sophisticated:** "I printed the poster in colour and in greyscale. In greyscale the orange subheading and red date merged into the same mid-grey, so I lightened the orange to create a clear value step. The headline's white-on-black contrast worked in both. The orange is now less saturated than I wanted, but the hierarchy survives photocopying, which the school office uses."
