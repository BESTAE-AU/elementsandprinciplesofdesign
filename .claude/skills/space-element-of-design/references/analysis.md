# Space: analysis and model answers

Use this file for analyses, annotations, exemplars and model answers about space in a specific design.

## Workflow

1. **Fix the artefact.** Work from the user's image or description. If you're inventing one for an exemplar, describe it briefly first so students can picture it, with the specific space characteristics the answers will cite.
2. **Work through the progression** (`method/analysis.md`): identify → describe → explain → analyse (connect to communication, purpose, audience) → evaluate against a named criterion, including a limitation or trade-off.
3. **For levelled exemplars** (e.g. developing / sound / high-range), make each level add a link in the reasoning chain, not just more words. Respect word limits. Under each, add a short note on what lifts it to the next level. The justification ladder in `feedback.md` shows the pattern.
4. **Go beyond the element on its own.** Use the relationships with other elements and principles in `knowledge.md`.
5. **Language** (`method/checks-and-language.md`): conditional wording for emotional or cultural readings, observation before interpretation, and no "it works" without a criterion.


## Worked analysis (model for exemplars and annotations)

Scenario: a school canteen menu board, 900 × 600 mm. Items are grouped in three columns (Hot, Cold, Drinks) with 60 mm gutters; each item and price sit on one line with 12 mm between items; a 100 mm band of empty space surrounds a "Meal deal" panel in the centre.

- **Identify:** "The menu uses space."
- **Describe:** "Three columns separated by 60 mm gutters; items spaced 12 mm apart within columns; a 100 mm clear zone round the central meal-deal panel."
- **Explain:** "The wide gutters and tight item spacing group items into three categories without needing divider lines; the clear zone isolates the meal deal so it's noticed first."
- **Analyse:** "Space works with proximity and hierarchy: each category reads as one unit, and isolation — not just size — gives the deal emphasis. Removing dividers lowers visual clutter for students scanning quickly in a queue."
- **Evaluate:** "Effective for fast scanning at 2–3 m in a queue. However, the 12 mm item spacing may be too tight for students with low vision reading from the back of the line; testing at the actual queue distance, and possibly dropping one item to open the spacing, would improve it."

Each level should add a link in the chain, not simply more words.
