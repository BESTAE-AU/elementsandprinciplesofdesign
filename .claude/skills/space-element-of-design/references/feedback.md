# Space: feedback and marking

Use this file when a teacher shares student work or a justification and wants feedback, a mark or "what gets them to the top band" on space.

## Workflow

1. **Read the work against its brief.** Note the audience, purpose and every touchpoint or output named (cups, embroidery, a phone screen, signage…). Reproduction problems usually hide there.
2. **Start from what the student actually did.** Quote their words or point to the part of the design, and name the space variable involved (Variables in `SKILL.md`).
3. **Check for the patterns below** and the misconceptions in `knowledge.md`.
4. **Check production and accessibility.** Use the production table in `knowledge.md` and `method/checks-and-language.md`.
5. **Place the reasoning on the justification ladder** (weak → developing → strong → sophisticated; `method/justification.md`). Say which link in the chain is missing.
6. **If marking:** use the rubric supplied. If none is supplied, use `marking-rubric-builder` (if available) to generate NESA-aligned criteria first, mark against them and say they were generated. Without that skill, give feedback without a grade (`method/feedback.md`).
7. **Write it up:**
   - **What's working:** specific, not generic.
   - **What's holding it back:** 2–4 points, each quoting the student.
   - **Next steps:** one or two, phrased as tests or design decisions, in priority order.
   - **Model structure** (optional): a fill-in-the-blanks version of a top-level justification, with a note not to copy the numbers.
   - **Teacher notes:** current level, gap to the top, and a conferencing question.

Keep the tone warm, honest and specific.


## Feedback patterns to look for in student work

- **Even spacing everywhere** — no grouping, so nothing belongs together.
- **Divider lines doing the job space should do.**
- **"Clean" or "not cluttered" as the whole justification.**
- **Crowded edges** — content hard against margins or trim.
- **Logo clear space ignored** in brand applications.
- **Physical clearance forgotten** in product or interior work (fingers, doors, wheelchair paths).

## Justification examples (Year 10 event flyer)

- **Weak:** "I left space so it looks clean."
- **Developing:** "I added more white space so the flyer isn't cluttered."
- **Strong:** "I grouped date, time and place tightly in one block with wide space around it, so the key details read as one unit and stand apart from the image. That suits parents glancing at a noticeboard."
- **Sophisticated:** "I compared my first version, where details were spread across the page, with a grouped version. In a 5-second test, 2 of 6 people found the time on the first; 6 of 6 on the grouped version. I kept the grouped layout but had to shrink the photo to make room — I accepted less visual impact for faster information."
