# Layout and composition: applying it in a design

This skill handles **applying it in a design** for layout and composition as an element of design. Everything known about layout and composition (definition, variables, types, relationships with the other elements and the principles, fields, production, misconceptions and worked examples) is in `references/element-knowledge.md`. The shared method is in `references/design-method.md`. Read the sections the workflow points to.

Related skills: `layout-and-composition-element-of-design-teaching`, `layout-and-composition-element-of-design-feedback`, `layout-and-composition-element-of-design-analysis`; `layout-and-composition-element-of-design` for general questions about layout and composition; the `*-principle-of-design` skills for principles.

Apply the language rules in `references/design-method.md` §14: Australian English, describe before interpreting, conditional language for emotional or cultural associations, and name the criterion whenever something "works".

## Workflow

1. **Clarify the brief.** Get the audience, purpose, every output or touchpoint, the production methods and any fixed constraints (brand rules, budget, equipment).
2. **Name the design question** before changing anything, e.g. "which weight keeps the mark readable at 16 px and in embroidery?". Don't vary things at random (method §7).
3. **Pick one variable** from the table below and propose three clearly different options. Hold the rest constant.
4. **Test in real conditions:** real size, real medium, real viewing distance or device. Check the production table below.
5. **If it's a brand,** extend the surviving options to at least three touchpoints and record what breaks away from the logo (method §8).
6. **Decide and record** the result as a rule (e.g. minimum sizes, permitted variants) and as a sophisticated justification: evidence → alternative rejected → trade-off accepted (method §4).

## Variables

| Variable | Range | Typical effect | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Placement** | position in the frame, page or screen | emphasis, relationships | Edges, crops and responsive reflow |
| **Alignment** | shared edges, axes, baselines | order, connection | Near-misses look accidental |
| **Proximity / grouping** | near ↔ far | structure | Back up critical groups with other cues |
| **Grid** | columns, rows, modules, baseline | consistency and flexibility | The grid should serve content, not dominate |
| **Margins / gutters** | edge and internal space | density, separation | Adapt for binding and device |
| **Visual weight** | relative prominence | balance, focal point | Weight comes from size, value, colour, texture, image and position |
| **Hierarchy** | primary, secondary, tertiary | organises attention | Must match content priorities |
| **Flow / reading order** | path through content | comprehension | Language and platform conventions |
| **Framing / cropping** | what's included or excluded | focus, relationships | Avoid accidental amputations and lost meaning |
| **Layering / overlap** | foreground–background | depth, grouping | Can reduce legibility |
| **Density / white space** | amount and distribution | cognitive load, pacing | Neither dense nor sparse is inherently better |
| **Repetition / rhythm** | recurring structures | consistency, progression | Variation should signal something |

## Production and reproduction

| Process | Layout risks |
|---|---|
| Print | Bleed, trim, binding, folds and imposition affect the final layout |
| Responsive digital | Breakpoints, reflow and dynamic content change composition |
| Video | Title-safe and action-safe areas and platform crops |
| Packaging | Dielines, folds, seams and panels fragment the visible layout |
| Textiles | Seams and cutting layouts break placement continuity |

## Layout and composition across fields

- **Branding:** brand systems need compositional rules — logo placement, margins, grid logic, image crops, graphic-device positions, text-to-image ratios and campaign templates. Consistent composition can be as recognisable as colour or type.
- **Graphic and communication design:** posters, publications, packaging, infographics and slides. Consider hierarchy, grouping, legibility, reading order, emphasis, consistency, scannability and efficiency.
- **Photography, film and animation:** framing, headroom, lead room, depth, blocking, focal points and multiple-subject balance. Motion graphics compose over time and across aspect ratios.
- **UI / UX:** responsive layouts must reflow, reorder and resize while keeping reading order, accessibility and component relationships. A desktop grid shouldn't just be squeezed onto mobile.
- **Textiles and fashion:** placement prints, motifs, embroidery, trims and panels compose the garment surface; placement must respect seams, darts, body movement and pattern matching.
- **Product and industrial design:** control placement, information panels, labels and graphics form functional layouts; reach, sequence and visibility often outweigh symmetry.
- **Interiors and architecture:** furniture, circulation, focal zones, lighting and architectural features compose space; evaluate use patterns, access and wayfinding.
- **Technical communication:** drawing sheets and diagrams need disciplined hierarchy, alignment, annotation placement and reading order; expressive composition must not compromise accuracy.
