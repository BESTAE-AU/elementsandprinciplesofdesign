# Multimedia production

Knowledge and teaching patterns for Multimedia. The essentials are below. Open **only** the reference file the task needs.

**Chain:** audience → purpose → content → media selection → interaction or sequence → production → delivery → testing. More media doesn't automatically improve communication; choose each medium for what it adds.

## Structured teaching page

When Britt asks for a structured content page (for Canvas), use exactly this structure:

1. **Title**
2. **Overview** (2–3 sentences)
3. **Core content**, as repeatable blocks. Each block: definition; why it matters; key ideas (3–5 points); guiding questions; video (topic plus 2–3 takeaways); image (description plus observations).
4. **Describing and analysing**: characteristics (appearance or sound), features (functionality), properties (technical details), attributes (audience, purpose, meaning).
5. **Additional resources**
6. **Example examination questions**: 4 multiple choice and 2 short answer.
7. **Application to Project**: what I will use; why I selected this; how it improves my project; alternative options considered.
8. **HSC-style questions**: 2 short and 2 extended.
9. **Glossary** (3–6 terms)

For other outputs (assessments, lesson plans, activities), use teacher-friendly formatting, include support and extension, align to the NSW syllabus where possible and keep it ready to copy into Canvas or a document. Don't explain what you're doing.

## Question rules

- Use HSC command verbs (identify, describe, explain, analyse, evaluate) and syllabus terminology.
- Extended responses integrate characteristics, features, properties and attributes.
- Questions target taught content and the identified syllabus version. "HSC-style" never means an official past paper.
- Keep resources tight: fewer, better questions over long lists.

## Analysis and justification

- **CFPA example:** a caption is a feature; its text size is a property; its appearance is a characteristic; better access to spoken information is an attribute.
- **Technique justification:** intended meaning → shot, sound or edit decision → perceptual effect → audience response → suitability.
- **Equipment choice:** equipment → capability → intended shot → project benefit → limitation → alternative. Judge what's available; a phone on a stable support can meet the brief.
- Judge techniques by their contribution to communication, not their count. Weak: "I used a dissolve because it looked good." Stronger: "The dissolve links two related stages while implying elapsed time; a direct cut was tested but suggested immediate continuation."

## Always

- Australian English. Verify software controls, platform specifications and export settings against current documentation (Adobe Help, platform pages) rather than stating fixed presets or limits.
- Build in captions and transcripts, reduced-motion alternatives, readable timing and text alternatives for essential visuals.
- Check permissions and licences for footage, music, fonts and people on camera.
