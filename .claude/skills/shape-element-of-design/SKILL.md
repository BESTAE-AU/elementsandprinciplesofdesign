## What shape is

Shape is a two-dimensional area defined by an edge, boundary, contrast, colour, value, texture or negative space. It can be geometric, organic, abstract, representational or symbolic, and can act as image, container, symbol, interface component, motif or pattern piece.

Keep it distinct from its neighbours:
- **Shape vs form:** shape is flat area; form is three-dimensional volume (actual or implied).
- **Shape vs contour:** a contour is the boundary of a shape or form, not the area itself.
- **Shape vs silhouette:** a silhouette is the recognisable outer shape of a subject — one reading of a form.
- **Shape vs outline:** shapes don't need outlines; contrast or negative space can define them.

## Variables

| Variable | Range | Typical effect | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Geometry** | geometric, organic, biomorphic, irregular | recognisability, precision, visual character | Avoid fixed personality claims |
| **Complexity** | simple → complex | recognition, detail, reproduction | Complex shapes fail small, cut or stitched |
| **Closure** | closed, open, implied | containment, figure-ground reading | Open shapes may be recognised more slowly |
| **Regularity / symmetry** | regular, symmetrical, irregular, asymmetric | predictability, rhythm, distinctiveness | Irregularity complicates systems |
| **Positive/negative relationship** | figure and surrounding or enclosed space | hidden forms, optical interplay | Check at small sizes and reversed out |
| **Edge character** | sharp, rounded, scalloped, rough, smooth | silhouette, tactility, production | Corner radii and gaps constrain cutting |
| **Proportion** | aspect ratio, relative dimensions | hierarchy, fit, stability | Test responsive and physical versions |
| **Overlap / intersection** | layered, merged, subtracted | depth, grouping, compound symbols | Boolean operations leave tiny slivers |
| **Repetition** | motif, tessellation, pattern | rhythm, identity | Test tiling, seams, crops |
