# Proportion: examples and teaching material

## Types and variations

- **Internal proportion:** parts of one object (lid to body).
- **Relational proportion:** elements to each other (heading to body text).
- **Human proportion:** designed to body dimensions.
- **Mathematical ratios:** golden ratio, 2:3, type scales, used as heuristics, not rules.
- **Exaggerated proportion:** deliberately distorted for effect (caricature, emphasis).

## Levers: what a designer changes to create proportion

- Ratios between parts
- Type scale
- Grid divisions
- Anthropometric data
- Deliberate exaggeration

## Common misconceptions

| Misconception | Better understanding |
|---|---|
| "The golden ratio makes designs beautiful." | It's one heuristic; many successful designs use other ratios. |
| "Proportion and scale are the same." | Scale references something outside; proportion is internal ratios. |
| "Correct proportion is universal." | Suitable proportion depends on use, users and context. |

## Worked analysis (model for exemplars and annotations)

Scenario: A kids' drink bottle: the cap is oversized relative to the body (about 1:3), with a chunky handle loop.

- **Identify:** "The design uses proportion."
- **Describe:** "The cap is about one-third the height of the body, larger than typical adult bottles; the handle is thick."
- **Explain:** "The large cap and handle suit small hands and make the bottle look playful."
- **Analyse:** "Proportion works with human scale and form: oversized parts match children's grip, and the exaggerated ratio also creates a friendly character."
- **Evaluate:** "Effective for young children. However, the tall cap raises the centre of gravity; a tip test on a lunchbox shelf is needed, and a wider base might restore stability."

Each level should add a link in the chain, not simply more words.

## Justification examples

- **Weak:** "I made it the right size."
- **Developing:** "I made the cap bigger so kids can open it."
- **Strong:** "I made the cap one-third of the body height and the handle thick, so children aged 4–7 can grip and open it, which also gives the bottle a playful look."
- **Sophisticated:** "I measured hand spans of eight children and tested 1:5 and 1:3 cap ratios. At 1:5, three couldn't open it. 1:3 worked for all but raised the tipping risk, so I widened the base by 6 mm."

## Feedback patterns to look for in student work

- **Proportion and scale confused.**
- **Golden ratio treated as a guarantee.**
- **No human data** for products and furniture.
- **Awkward ratios** between heading and body text.

## Activity ideas for teaching resources

- **Ratio hunt:** measure the ratios in three logos or products.
- **Stretch test:** distort a design's proportions and discuss what changes.
- **Hand-span data (D&T):** collect class measurements and set a handle proportion.
- **Type scale:** set a heading/body ratio at 1.25, 1.5 and 2 and compare.

## What each element contributes

- **Colour:** the ratio of each colour sets the overall reading.
- **Form:** relationships between parts (lid to body, handle to vessel), and between object and user.
- **Line:** line length relative to the format, and the stroke-to-size ratio in icons and marks.
- **Shape:** internal proportions of a shape, and the ratio between shapes.
- **Size and scale:** internal ratios (logo mark to wordmark, head to body in illustration, seat to table).
