## What emphasis is

Emphasis is making one part of a design dominant so it's noticed first. It creates a focal point (and sometimes secondary accents) by making something different from its surroundings.

Keep it distinct:
- **Emphasis vs hierarchy:** emphasis is the focal point; hierarchy is the full ranked order of everything.
- **Emphasis vs contrast:** contrast is a main way to create emphasis, but isolation, placement and convergence also work.
- **Emphasis vs clutter:** emphasising everything emphasises nothing.

## Types and variations

- **Emphasis by contrast:** value, colour, size, shape or texture difference.
- **Emphasis by isolation:** one element surrounded by space.
- **Emphasis by placement:** at a strong point, the centre or the end of a path.
- **Emphasis by convergence:** lines, gazes or perspective pointing at it.
- **Emphasis by the unexpected:** one element breaking a pattern.
- **Emphasis in time:** a hold, a zoom, a sound cue.

## Levers: what a designer changes to create emphasis

- Contrast (any kind)
- Isolation in space
- Placement
- Converging lines and gaze
- Pattern-breaking
- Motion and timing

## What each element contributes

- **Colour:** a single saturated accent in a muted field creates a focal point.
- **Direction:** converging vectors and gaze lines point to a focal point.
- **Form:** a distinctive feature form (a sculpted handle, a cantilever) becomes the focal point.
- **Layout and composition:** placement, scale and isolation create a clear first, second and third read.
- **Line:** lines converging on a point, a single heavy rule among light ones, or an underline create a focal point.
- **Shape:** an unusual or larger shape becomes the focal point.
- **Size and scale:** scale contrast (one very large element) creates a focal point.
- **Space:** isolation in space creates a focal point.
- **Texture:** the one textured area in a plain design draws the eye (or hand).
- **Time and duration:** a hold, a slow-motion moment or a pause before a reveal.
- **Typography:** a single change (bold, colour, size) highlights a word; too many cancel out.
- **Value:** the lightest light against the darkest dark usually becomes the focal point.
