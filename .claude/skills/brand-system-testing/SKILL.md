# Brand-system testing

A brand is a **system of repeatable rules**, not a single artwork. Distinctive brand assets (a colour, a shape language, a line style, a crop, a motion behaviour) can exist independently of the logo. This skill tests elements across touchpoints and writes the results as guidelines. The shared method is in `references/design-method.md` (§8 matters most). Each `<element>-element-of-design` skill has a branding section for that element.

## Workflow

1. **Define the concept and audience.** Keep them constant while testing; vary only the element under test.
2. **Test in the primary mark or graphic device first.**
3. **Extend each surviving option to at least three touchpoints,** chosen from:
   - packaging
   - social post and avatar
   - website header or app icon
   - signage
   - merchandise (e.g. embroidery)
   - print ad
   - environmental graphics
   - motion or video
4. **Test the edge cases:**
   - smallest size (favicon, avatar, cup stamp) and largest (signage)
   - positive and reversed
   - monochrome and greyscale
   - different production methods (print, screen, embroidery, vinyl, engraving)
   - light and dark backgrounds
5. **Record what breaks** away from the logo, and why.
6. **Write the rule.** Refine the brand rule, not just one artwork.

## What guidelines should specify

- Logo versions: primary, secondary, symbol-only, small-size, monochrome and reversed.
- Minimum sizes, set from real tests, not guesses.
- Clear space, in relative units (e.g. the height of one letter).
- Colour system: roles, proportions, accessible pairings, and RGB/HEX, CMYK and Pantone.
- Typography hierarchy, weights, fallbacks and licensing.
- Graphic devices: line or stroke rules, shape and corner logic, patterns, image crops.
- Spacing system and layout templates.
- Image treatment.
- Motion behaviour (durations, easing, entry direction) with reduced-motion versions.
- Production notes: embroidery, vinyl and print limits.
- Do's and don'ts, with examples across touchpoints.

## Common weaknesses

- Brand work that stops at the logo.
- One logo size for everything.
- Colours specified in one format only.
- No monochrome version.
- Minimum sizes guessed, not tested.
- Guidelines that show only the ideal case.

## Teaching ideas

- **Break the logo:** apply a student logo to six touchpoints and find where it fails.
- **Guideline audit:** compare a real brand's guidelines with its actual use in the wild.
- **Asset without logo:** identify a brand from a crop that contains no logo.
