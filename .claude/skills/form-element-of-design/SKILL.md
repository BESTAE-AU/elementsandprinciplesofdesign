## What form is

Form is the three-dimensional quality, volume or physical configuration of an object or space: mass, void, surface, edge, plane, profile and depth. In 2D media, form is implied through value, perspective, overlap, texture, contour and lighting.

Keep it distinct from its neighbours:
- **Form vs shape:** shape is a flat area; form occupies or implies volume.
- **Actual vs implied form:** a product has actual form; a drawing or render of it implies form.
- **Form vs silhouette:** a silhouette shows the outer profile only, not all surfaces or internal structure.
- **Form vs texture:** surface is part of form, but texture is a separate element.

## Variables

| Variable | Range | Typical effect | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mass and volume** | solid, hollow, open, enclosed | perceived weight, capacity, structural behaviour | Visual mass vs actual mass and material |
| **Geometry** | geometric, organic, biomorphic | construction, perception, manufacture | Avoid universal emotional readings |
| **Complexity** | simple, compound, articulated | usability, tooling, assembly | More complexity can add cost and failure points |
| **Curvature and planes** | flat, faceted, single or compound curves | grip, reflection, strength, tooling | Compound curves need complex forming |
| **Edge transitions** | sharp, chamfered, filleted, blended | safety, wear, tactility, appearance | Radii can be manufacturing-critical |
| **Proportion** | relationships between dimensions | balance, fit, human interaction | Use anthropometric evidence |
| **Surface** | continuous, segmented, perforated | tactile and visual behaviour | Texture is a separate element |
| **Void** | openings, cavities, negative volume | lightness, access, ventilation, handles | Check structure, cleaning, safety |
| **Light and shadow** | modelled, flat, dramatic, diffuse | reveals or hides form in images | Lighting changes perception, not physical form |
