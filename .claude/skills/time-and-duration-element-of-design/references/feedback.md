# Time and duration: feedback and marking

Use this file when a teacher shares student work or a justification and wants feedback, a mark or "what gets them to the top band" on time and duration.

## Workflow

1. **Read the work against its brief.** Note the audience, purpose and every touchpoint or output named (cups, embroidery, a phone screen, signage…). Reproduction problems usually hide there.
2. **Start from what the student actually did.** Quote their words or point to the part of the design, and name the time and duration variable involved (Variables in `SKILL.md`).
3. **Check for the patterns below** and the misconceptions in `knowledge.md`.
4. **Check production and accessibility.** Use the production table in `knowledge.md` and `method/checks-and-language.md`.
5. **Place the reasoning on the justification ladder** (weak → developing → strong → sophisticated; `method/justification.md`). Say which link in the chain is missing.
6. **If marking:** use the rubric supplied. If none is supplied, use `marking-rubric-builder` (if available) to generate NESA-aligned criteria first, mark against them and say they were generated. Without that skill, give feedback without a grade (`method/feedback.md`).
7. **Write it up:**
   - **What's working:** specific, not generic.
   - **What's holding it back:** 2–4 points, each quoting the student.
   - **Next steps:** one or two, phrased as tests or design decisions, in priority order.
   - **Model structure** (optional): a fill-in-the-blanks version of a top-level justification, with a note not to copy the numbers.
   - **Teacher notes:** current level, gap to the top, and a conferencing question.

Keep the tone warm, honest and specific.


## Feedback patterns to look for in student work

- **Text on screen too briefly** to read, or moving while it should be read.
- **Every shot the same length** — no pacing variation.
- **Decorative transitions** (spins, page curls) with no purpose.
- **Linear easing everywhere,** so motion feels mechanical.
- **Audio and visual out of sync,** or meaning dependent on sound alone.
- **Auto-advancing or looping content** with no pause control, or no reduced-motion option.

## Justification examples (Year 10 logo animation)

- **Weak:** "I made the animation fast because it looks cool."
- **Developing:** "I made the logo animate in quickly so it feels energetic."
- **Strong:** "I animated the logo in over 0.8 s with an ease-out, so it arrives quickly and settles gently, then held it for 2 s so viewers can read the name. That suits a 6-second social ad for a skate shop where people scroll fast."
- **Sophisticated:** "I compared 0.4 s, 0.8 s and 1.5 s entries with five classmates. At 0.4 s two didn't notice the logo arrive; at 1.5 s it felt slow and ate into the hold. 0.8 s with an ease-out was noticed by all five and left 2 s to read. I also made a static version for users with reduced-motion settings. I gave up some drama for readability."
