# Time and duration: knowledge

The definition and variables are in `../SKILL.md`.

## Types

- **Linear time** — fixed beginning-to-end sequence; film, video, slideshows.
- **Interactive time** — user-controlled timing and order; UI, games, installations.
- **Cyclical / looped time** — repeating sequence; motion identities, GIFs, ambient displays.
- **Simultaneous time** — several events at once; split screen, dashboards, layered motion.
- **Asynchronous time** — independent events; notifications, distributed media.
- **Represented time** — time implied in static work; storyboards, comic panels, motion blur in a photo.

## How time and duration work with the other elements

- **Line:** lines draw on, trace and reveal; the speed of a line's growth sets pace.
- **Direction:** entry side, travel path, exit and consistent screen direction across cuts.
- **Shape:** shapes morph, mask and transition; recognition must survive the change.
- **Form:** turntables, camera moves round 3D forms, products that unfold.
- **Space:** motion needs room — entry and exit space; pauses are temporal "space" that let content breathe.
- **Size and scale:** zooms, scale-ups and shot-size changes over time.
- **Colour:** colour changes through a sequence (grade shifts, state colours); rapid flashing is an accessibility risk.
- **Value:** fades to and from black or white; light changes across a scene.
- **Texture:** grain and noise animate; compression can smear texture in fast motion.
- **Typography:** reading time is the hard constraint on titles, captions and kinetic type.
- **Layout and composition:** composition changes over time — hierarchy shifts as elements enter and leave.

## How time and duration contribute to the principles

- **Rhythm:** edit and animation rhythm — regular cuts, beats, repeated intervals.
- **Emphasis:** a hold, a slow-motion moment or a pause before a reveal.
- **Hierarchy:** order of appearance ranks information (what arrives first reads first).
- **Contrast:** a sudden change of pace (fast montage to a long still shot).
- **Unity:** consistent durations and easing across a motion system feel like one brand.
- **Movement:** timing and easing make movement read as heavy, light, mechanical or natural.
- **Tension:** delay, withholding and extended duration build suspense.
- **Balance:** alternating fast and slow sections balance a sequence.

## Time and duration across fields

- **Branding:** a motion identity specifies logo animation, entry and exit durations, easing families, transition behaviour, sequencing and tempo, with reduced-motion and static equivalents.
- **Graphic and communication design:** mostly static, but time appears in sequence (multi-panel posters, carousels, slide decks) and in how long a viewer has to read (billboards, bus sides).
- **Video, film and animation:** shot duration, edit pacing, montage, continuity, frame timing, keyframes, holds, spacing, anticipation, easing. Motion graphics coordinate entrance, hold, transition and exit so text is readable before it goes.
- **UI / UX:** response time, feedback delay, loading states, microinteraction duration, progressive disclosure and timeouts. Users should be able to pause, stop or extend time; avoid auto-advancing essential content (WCAG 2.2.2).
- **Textiles and fashion:** less central to a static textile, but relevant to wear, transformation, responsive textiles, performance costumes and production sequence.
- **Product and industrial design:** interaction feedback, cycle times, maintenance intervals, staged use. Latency changes perceived quality and safety.
- **Interiors, exhibitions and architecture:** visitor sequence, dwell time, queues, timed media, daylight change and programmed installations.
- **Technical communication:** storyboards, animatics, timelines, cue sheets and state diagrams communicate timing; a static drawing can't show duration without notation.

## Production and reproduction

| Process | Time risks |
|---|---|
| Video | Frame rate, platform length limits and compression constrain timing |
| Animation | Frame timing and render cost limit motion complexity |
| UI | Device performance and latency change intended transition timing |
| Exhibitions | Scheduling, dwell, reset time and accessibility shape timing |
| Social platforms | Autoplay without sound, loop behaviour and first-second hooks |

## Common misconceptions

| Misconception | Better understanding |
|---|---|
| "Time is just video length." | It includes sequence, timing, pauses, tempo, response and user control. |
| "Fast pacing is more engaging." | Pacing must match content, audience and processing demands. Test comprehension. |
| "Duration and speed are the same." | Duration is elapsed time; speed is rate of change. Describe both. |
| "Transitions make a video look professional." | Unmotivated transitions distract; a clean cut is often best. |
| "If I can read it, everyone can." | The maker knows the text already. Test with a first-time viewer. |
