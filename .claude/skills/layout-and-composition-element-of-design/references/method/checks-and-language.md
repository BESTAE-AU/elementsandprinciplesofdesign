# Design method: Checks for every task: accessibility, production, sustainability, culture, language rules and final check

Part of the shared method used by every Elements and Principles of Design skill. The other parts sit alongside this file in `references/method/`.

## Checks that apply to every element

**Accessibility** — build it into the decision, not after it.
- Is essential information carried by more than one cue (not colour alone, not a hairline alone)?
- Does it survive zoom, reduction, reflow, greyscale printing and different screens?
- Does it add unnecessary visual or cognitive load?
- Where motion is involved, can it respect reduced-motion preferences?
- For physical work: grip, reach, tactile cues, sensory comfort, safe movement.

**Production and reproduction** — don't invent universal minimum sizes, weights or tolerances. They depend on equipment, substrate, process, final size, viewing distance, material behaviour, tooling, resolution and supplier specifications. Recommend proofs and tests wherever production could change the result. For type-heavy or cross-platform work, also consider licensing, language support, available weights and widths, and how assets resize across formats.

**Sustainability** — don't assume that minimal = sustainable, fewer colours = sustainable, digital = sustainable, or natural material = sustainable. Ask whether the treatment changes materials, inks, energy, set-up waste, rejects, maintenance or product life, and whether a trend-driven choice will force early redesign. Weigh environmental, social and economic effects.

**Culture and ethics** — separate common associations from culturally specific meanings; do not treat Western reading direction or symbolism as universal; where a design draws on a specific culture, recommend research with or about that community rather than guessing.

## Language rules

- Australian English.
- Describe before interpreting.
- Use conditional language for emotional, cultural and perceptual associations: *may suggest*, *can be read as*, *in this context*.
- Never present preference as justification. Avoid "looks good", "makes it pop", unsupported aesthetic judgements and emotion claims stated as universal facts.
- Prefer precise, concise student language to inflated responses.
- Name the criterion when saying something "works".
- Keep elements (variables) and principles (relationships) distinct: an element *contributes to* a principle; it does not automatically create it.
- No body-value judgements or stereotypes.
- Preferred verbs: contributes to, reinforces, establishes, directs, emphasises, separates, groups, connects, differentiates, compromises, obscures.

## Final check

Before handing over any resource, analysis or feedback:

- Claims of effect trace back to an observable characteristic.
- Variables are named precisely.
- Relevant (not all) Factors Affecting Design are used.
- Accessibility and production/reproduction are considered where they matter.
- Justifications include purpose, audience/context and suitability; sophisticated ones include evidence, an alternative and a trade-off.
- Branding is treated as a system across touchpoints; distinctive brand assets can exist beyond the logo.
- Sustainability and cultural claims are evidence-based and not universalised.
- Trade-offs are explicit and refinement actions are specific.
- Static and time-based uses are distinguished where relevant.
- Australian English throughout.
