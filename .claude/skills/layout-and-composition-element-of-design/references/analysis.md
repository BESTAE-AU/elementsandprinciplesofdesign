# Layout and composition: analysis and model answers

Use this file for analyses, annotations, exemplars and model answers about layout and composition in a specific design.

## Workflow

1. **Fix the artefact.** Work from the user's image or description. If you're inventing one for an exemplar, describe it briefly first so students can picture it, with the specific layout and composition characteristics the answers will cite.
2. **Work through the progression** (`method/analysis.md`): identify → describe → explain → analyse (connect to communication, purpose, audience) → evaluate against a named criterion, including a limitation or trade-off.
3. **For levelled exemplars** (e.g. developing / sound / high-range), make each level add a link in the reasoning chain, not just more words. Respect word limits. Under each, add a short note on what lifts it to the next level. The justification ladder in `feedback.md` shows the pattern.
4. **Go beyond the element on its own.** Use the relationships with other elements and principles in `knowledge.md`.
5. **Language** (`method/checks-and-language.md`): conditional wording for emotional or cultural readings, observation before interpretation, and no "it works" without a criterion.


## Worked analysis (model for exemplars and annotations)

Scenario: a double-page magazine spread on a local skate park. A full-bleed photo of a skater mid-air fills the left page and crosses the gutter by 40 mm; the right page has a large headline top-left, a short standfirst, and body text in two columns on a 6-column grid, with a pull quote spanning two columns.

- **Identify:** "The spread uses layout and composition."
- **Describe:** "Full-bleed photo on the left page crossing 40 mm over the gutter; on the right, a headline top-left, a standfirst, two columns of body on a 6-column grid, and a pull quote spanning two columns."
- **Explain:** "The large photo is the first read and gives energy; the headline, aligned to the grid, is the second read; the columns and pull quote organise the text into manageable blocks."
- **Analyse:** "Composition works with scale, alignment and direction: the skater faces right, into the page, so their implied direction leads the eye to the headline. The photo crossing the gutter joins the two pages into one composition, and the consistent grid keeps the text side orderly against the photo's energy — a deliberate asymmetrical balance."
- **Evaluate:** "Effective for a lifestyle magazine where readers are drawn in by the image and then read. However, the 40 mm crossover risks losing part of the skater's hand in the binding; the image should be checked against the printer's gutter allowance, or shifted so no key detail falls in the fold."

Each level should add a link in the chain, not simply more words.
