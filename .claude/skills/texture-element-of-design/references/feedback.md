# Texture: feedback and marking

Use this file when a teacher shares student work or a justification and wants feedback, a mark or "what gets them to the top band" on texture.

## Workflow

1. **Read the work against its brief.** Note the audience, purpose and every touchpoint or output named (cups, embroidery, a phone screen, signage…). Reproduction problems usually hide there.
2. **Start from what the student actually did.** Quote their words or point to the part of the design, and name the texture variable involved (Variables in `SKILL.md`).
3. **Check for the patterns below** and the misconceptions in `knowledge.md`.
4. **Check production and accessibility.** Use the production table in `knowledge.md` and `method/checks-and-language.md`.
5. **Place the reasoning on the justification ladder** (weak → developing → strong → sophisticated; `method/justification.md`). Say which link in the chain is missing.
6. **If marking:** use the rubric supplied. If none is supplied, use `marking-rubric-builder` (if available) to generate NESA-aligned criteria first, mark against them and say they were generated. Without that skill, give feedback without a grade (`method/feedback.md`).
7. **Write it up:**
   - **What's working:** specific, not generic.
   - **What's holding it back:** 2–4 points, each quoting the student.
   - **Next steps:** one or two, phrased as tests or design decisions, in priority order.
   - **Model structure** (optional): a fill-in-the-blanks version of a top-level justification, with a note not to copy the numbers.
   - **Teacher notes:** current level, gap to the top, and a conferencing question.

Keep the tone warm, honest and specific.


## Feedback patterns to look for in student work

- **Actual and visual texture not distinguished** in written analysis.
- **Texture as decoration only** — no link to touch, grip, durability or care.
- **Text placed on busy texture** with poor legibility.
- **Overlays used to hide weak composition.**
- **Finish chosen without testing** real material (screen mock-up only).
- **Care and durability ignored** in textiles and products.

## Justification examples (Year 10 Textiles cushion)

- **Weak:** "I used velvet because it's soft and nice."
- **Developing:** "I used velvet so the cushion feels soft and cosy."
- **Strong:** "I used a cotton velvet front and a plain canvas back. The velvet's pile gives a soft touch for the side people lean on, while the canvas is harder-wearing on the side that sits against the couch. That suits a family lounge cushion that will be used every day."
- **Sophisticated:** "I compared cotton velvet, polyester velvet and corduroy after 20 rubs with a scouring pad and one wash. The polyester pilled; the corduroy kept its texture but felt stiffer. Cotton velvet crushed slightly but recovered with steaming, so I chose it and added a care label. It costs more than polyester, but it lasts longer in daily use."
