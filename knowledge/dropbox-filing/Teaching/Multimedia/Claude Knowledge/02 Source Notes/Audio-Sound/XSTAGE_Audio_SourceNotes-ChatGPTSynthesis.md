## Sound as communication

Audio production captures or creates sound; post-production edits, processes, mixes and delivers it. Sound can establish information, atmosphere, emotion, rhythm, space and continuity. Its role should be planned with the intended audience and listening conditions.

## Vocabulary and distinctions

- Dialogue: spoken performance or interview content.
- Voiceover: narration not necessarily spoken by a visible person.
- Foley: performed sound recreated to match actions.
- ADR: re-recorded dialogue matched to a performance.
- Ambience: background sound establishing the environment.
- Sound effects: designed or recorded events.
- MIDI: event/control information, not an audio recording.
- Loop: a repeatable sound segment; check seams and licences.
- Equalisation: frequency-dependent level adjustment.
- Compression: dynamic-range control; distinct from file compression.
- Panning: placement within a stereo or multichannel field.

## Recording decisions

Microphone directionality, distance, location acoustics, handling noise, wind, connections and monitoring affect results. Microphone type alone does not establish suitability. A close, correctly placed modest microphone can outperform a distant expensive one.

Record a test and listen through headphones. Leave headroom to avoid clipping; no single peak target suits every device and format. Capture room tone when useful for matching edits. Plan consent, background voices, power and storage.

Sample rate describes samples per second; bit depth affects quantisation and recording headroom characteristics. Higher numbers do not repair noise or poor placement. WAV commonly supports uncompressed audio; MP3 and AAC commonly use lossy coding. Check the actual encoding rather than extension alone.

## Post-production sequence

**Organise → select → edit → clean → balance → process → automate → deliver → listen.**

Prioritise intelligibility. Noise reduction can introduce artefacts; prevent problems during capture where possible. EQ may reduce masking but should be judged by listening. Loudness, peaks and dynamic range are different measures; use the delivery specification rather than inventing one universal loudness target.

Music can support pacing or overwhelm speech. Ducking reduces competing sound while dialogue occurs. Sound bridges support continuity; silence can provide emphasis. Always compare the mix on realistic speakers as well as headphones.

## Accessibility, ethics and teaching

Captions represent speech and meaningful sounds in timed text; transcripts provide a reading alternative. Automated captions require correction. A transcript alone does not make a synchronised video meet every applicable caption requirement. Identify music/sound licences and attribution obligations before distribution.

Support students through a short recorded explanation and provided sound layers. Extend with original Foley, a sound-only narrative or alternative mixes for listening contexts. Assess clarity, purposeful sound relationships, technical quality and evidence of refinement.

Justification framework: **sound choice → audible effect → narrative/communication function → listener context → suitability**. Avoid equating louder with clearer or more professional.
