# Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander education

## Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander education

Aboriginal Peoples and Torres Strait Islander Peoples are diverse, living and contemporary communities with distinct identities, knowledge and relationships. Avoid a single generic culture or treating Indigenous knowledge only as historical.

Meaningful integration involves accurate Indigenous-authored resources, local relationships, contextual knowledge, appropriate authority and sustained learning. Indigenous students should not be expected to represent or explain all Indigenous cultures.

## Curriculum and knowledge relationships

ACARA's [cross-curriculum priority](https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/curriculum-information/understand-this-cross-curriculum-priority/aboriginal-and-torres-strait-islander-histories-and-cultures) uses Country/Place, Culture and People as organising aspects. Apply the relevant NSW syllabus content explicitly rather than assuming all subjects require identical activities.

TAS investigations may consider material selection, weaving, tools, design, technologies and relationships with Country. Visual Arts may investigate artists' practices and representation with appropriate sources. Avoid reducing knowledge to craft patterns detached from people and place.

## Eight Ways of Learning

The [8 Ways framework](https://www.8ways.online/) describes interconnected pedagogies: story sharing, learning maps, non-verbal learning, symbols and images, land links, non-linear learning, deconstruct/reconstruct and community links. It is a situated pedagogical framework, not a list of learning styles or a universal description of all Indigenous learners.

Use [its protocols](https://www.8ways.online/our-protocol) and engage with community to understand local ways of teaching and learning. Do not mechanically require eight activities to claim implementation.

## Cultural protocols and ICIP

Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property concerns cultural knowledge and expression, including responsibilities that may extend beyond ordinary copyright. Public availability does not establish permission to reproduce, commercialise, alter or feed material into AI systems.

Determine who has authority, what may be shared, how attribution works, whether consent is needed, what restrictions apply and how benefits or compensation are addressed. Do not invent cultural permission or local endorsement.

Evaluate resources for authorship, accuracy, representation, historical framing, stereotypes and cultural suitability. The [AIATSIS education resource guide](https://aiatsis.gov.au/education/guide-evaluating-and-selecting-education-resources) supports reflection and selection.

## Cross-curricular project planning

**Local relationship → agreed learning → authoritative resources → subject links → activities → reflection → continuing relationship.**

A weaving workshop should be planned with the invited practitioner: technique, context, materials, permissions, appropriate adaptations, payment, attribution and follow-up. The spelling and identity of any practitioner must be confirmed before publication; do not resolve an unclear remembered name by guessing.

Years 7 and 8 can have related inquiries with different subject demands. A shared cultural focus does not justify claiming syllabus coverage without exact content mapping.

## Teaching and assessment

Assess respectful inquiry, accurate understanding, subject skills and evidence-informed reflection rather than imitation of cultural motifs. Support with carefully selected sources and guided questions. Extend through contemporary Indigenous designers, systems knowledge and critique of representation.

Tokenism includes isolated themed activities, generic “Aboriginal-style” imagery or assuming all Indigenous cultures use the same symbols. Stronger practice sustains relationships, specific attribution and learning about context and responsibilities.


## Resource and workshop planning record

Record the creator/practitioner, Country/community where appropriately identified, content source, authority for sharing, intended student learning, subject link, permissions, attribution, materials, cost and follow-up. Include uncertainty rather than fill a gap with invented information.

Do not require a student to disclose identity, family knowledge or cultural practices as part of demonstrating learning. Offer meaningful participation without turning personal identity into a classroom resource.

## Differentiating Year 7 and Year 8 work

Both cohorts might engage with a shared practitioner and contextual learning, while responding through different subject skills. One might document observation and material experimentation; another might compare processes, develop a design proposal or analyse a system. Those examples become curriculum claims only after exact Stage 4 subject content has been verified.

Evaluate the relationship and learning beyond the final craft product. Ask whether students understood the specific context, responsibilities and contemporary relevance, and whether the practitioner/community had a genuine role in shaping the experience.
