# Branding and visual identity

## Brand, identity and visual identity

A brand comprises associations and experiences connected with an organisation, product or practice. Brand identity describes intended positioning and expression. Visual identity is the visible system: logos, colour, type, imagery, graphic devices, layout and motion. A logo is one asset within that system.

**Purpose/context → audience → positioning → personality → identity system → touchpoints → evaluation.**

Strategy should precede a rationale for colour or logo style. Recognition is helped by consistency; consistency means stable relationships and rules, not identical layouts everywhere.

## System components

- Logo variants: primary, secondary, symbol, wordmark, monochrome and responsive variants where justified.
- Lock-ups: controlled relationships between symbol, name and supporting text.
- Clear space and minimum sizes: derive from actual legibility and reproduction testing; do not invent universal dimensions.
- Colour roles: primary, secondary, neutral, background, emphasis and functional states.
- Typography: families, weights, hierarchy, language coverage and licensing.
- Graphic language: shapes, lines, textures, icons, grids and spacing.
- Image treatment: subject selection, framing, tonal treatment and consistency.
- Motion identity: pacing, transition character, logo behaviour and reduced-motion equivalents.
- Templates: reusable structures that allow content changes without breaking identity.

Brand architecture explains relationships among parent brands, sub-brands and offerings. A touchpoint is an encounter with the brand: website, publication, packaging, signage, merchandise, social content or service experience.

## Auditing and testing

Assess hierarchy, recognisability, consistency, accessibility and purpose across several touchpoints. Distinguish approved variation from drift. Record evidence, location, severity, suggested correction and likely effect.

Test logos at small scale, monochrome, reversed colour, on photographs and across production methods. Test typography and palette in real content, not only isolated swatches. Fonts must support intended languages and licences: [Adobe Fonts licensing](https://helpx.adobe.com/fonts/web/font-licensing/font-licensing.html).

## Teaching applications

Students develop positioning and audience before visual alternatives, then compare identity systems rather than isolated logos. Scaffold **choice → effect → brand personality → audience → suitability**. Extend by creating responsive variants and identifying trade-offs between novelty and recognisability.

Common misconception: “Consistency means every post is identical.” Better interpretation: consistent assets and relationships permit purposeful layout variation.
