## Curriculum and program writing

A syllabus specifies intended learning and course requirements. A program explains planned teaching, learning, assessment and resources. A scope and sequence maps progression and timing across units. A lesson sequence connects instruction and practice within a unit.

**Authoritative syllabus/version → outcomes/content → evidence → sequence → strategies/resources → assessment → evaluation.**

## Mapping and alignment

Record outcome codes and exact text separately from paraphrases. Identify content taught, practised and assessed. Do not infer coverage from merely mentioning an outcome. Learning intentions express the intended learning; success criteria describe recognisable achievement appropriate to the task.

Backward design identifies acceptable evidence before activities. Constructive alignment tests whether students have opportunities to learn what assessment expects. Sequence conceptual and practical prerequisites, allow guided practice and include review and transfer.

## Program components

Include course, stage/year, syllabus edition, unit title, duration, rationale, outcomes, content, learning sequence, teaching strategies, differentiation, literacy/numeracy, resources, safety, assessment and evaluation. Distinguish an assessment opportunity from a formal summative task.

Practical project programs should connect investigation, criteria, ideas, testing, production and evaluation. A product alone does not define a unit; explain the learning the product enables.

## Cross-curricular planning

Start with meaningful shared inquiry and identify each subject's actual contribution. Cross-curriculum priorities and general capabilities enrich learning but do not replace subject requirements. Indigenous integration requires appropriate local relationships and resources, not thematic decoration.

## Differentiation and evaluation

State specific support and extension within activities: modelling, vocabulary, visuals, guided comparison, alternative access, independent inquiry and competing constraints. Avoid a generic “differentiate as needed” column detached from the learning.

Evaluate evidence of student learning, pace, barriers, resource suitability, safety and assessment alignment. Record revisions for the next implementation. Programs should be usable by another teacher without relying on unstated assumptions.

The MCCNS plugin provides no substantive instructions in this session; do not infer an official school template or policy from it.


## A practical unit-writing sequence

1. Resolve course, stage, cohort and governing syllabus edition.
2. Identify exact outcomes/content and relevant course requirements.
3. Establish the culminating learning evidence and realistic task.
4. Map prerequisites and the conceptual/practical sequence.
5. Design explicit instruction, inquiry and guided/independent practice.
6. Integrate formative checks, differentiation, literacy/numeracy and approved safety procedures.
7. Allocate resources, timing and assessment checkpoints.
8. Verify alignment, coverage, accessibility and feasibility.
9. Record implementation evidence and revisions.

This describes a planning relationship, not an installable agent workflow.

## Common mapping errors

Copying every outcome into every lesson hides actual coverage. Listing “critical thinking” does not identify the reasoning students undertake. A recycled-material project does not automatically satisfy all sustainability content. A cultural workshop does not establish cross-curricular coverage without subject-specific evidence.

Prefer explicit relationships: a particular content point is taught through a defined investigation, practised through a comparison and assessed through a justification or performance. Keep official syllabus text distinguishable from the teacher's activity description.
