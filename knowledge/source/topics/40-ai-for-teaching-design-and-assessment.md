# AI for Teaching, Design and Assessment

## Purpose and provenance

A reference knowledge base for Claude, covering concepts, relationships, application and teaching. This is not an installable skill.

General subject knowledge synthesised for this collection; relevant plugin instructions and verified primary-source links are identified in the collection references.

## AI literacy and educational purpose

Generative AI produces content based on learned patterns; fluent output does not establish truth, authority or appropriateness. AI literacy involves understanding capabilities and limits, evaluating output, making responsible decisions and retaining human judgement.

**Learning/design purpose → permitted use → relevant context → generation → verification → revision → documented use.**

An AI task should support learning or work without obscuring who made decisions. AI can assist ideation, examples, scaffolds, wording, code and feedback, but can also fabricate citations, misread visual evidence or create plausible errors.

## Knowledge bases and skills

A knowledge base records concepts, relationships, examples, terminology and source limits. A skill defines when and how to carry out work, including tools, steps and validation. This collection provides knowledge bases; a separate creator can later derive skill behaviour.

Keep durable knowledge separate from temporary project facts, current platform rules and credentials. Retrieval can provide relevant context, but retrieved snippets still require interpretation and source checking.

## Prompt structure

State purpose, audience, context, relevant sources, desired evidence, output format and constraints. Ask for uncertainty to be identified and facts to be linked to sources. Examples can clarify style without prescribing the entire solution.

For Claude Code, supply the smallest relevant topic files plus project material. Avoid loading the entire collection for a narrow task. Preserve original source files and create reviewable outputs. Define which files are authoritative when versions conflict.

## Teaching and design uses

AI can generate alternatives, but selection must connect to purpose and evidence. In Visual Arts, document the student's concept, transformations and decisions. In practical instruction, verify sequence, equipment and settings against authoritative guidance. In curriculum work, use actual syllabus text and version.

AI-generated cultural content must not invent community permission, restricted knowledge or Indigenous symbolism. Do not use generic style imitation as a substitute for relationships and authoritative resources.

## Assessment and feedback

Define permitted AI assistance according to the actual task and school policy. Teach attribution and evidence of process. Writing style or detector output alone does not establish misconduct.

AI feedback must refer to inspected work and the rubric, distinguish inaccessible/missing/partial evidence and identify provisional judgements. Final marking and consequential decisions remain subject to teacher review and authorised procedures.

## Privacy and governance

Use approved systems and minimise identified student data. Do not assume every service has the same data handling, age conditions or school approval. Verify current policy before uploading sensitive work or deploying an agent.

The [Australian Framework for Generative AI in Schools](https://www.education.gov.au/schooling/resources/australian-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence-ai-schools) provides national guidance for responsible use. It does not replace a school's policy or authorise a particular platform.

## Agent workflows and quality

Separate retrieval, analysis, drafting and action. Preserve evidence locations and versions. Check outputs before publication, messages or changes to live systems. Preparing a draft does not authorise sending it.

For portfolio auditing, trace every judgement to evidence. For Canvas development, verify HTML, links, student access and learning sequence. For resource generation, check accuracy, access, task demand and layout.

Evaluate AI assistance by correctness, usefulness, teacher control, student learning, access and time saved after verification—not output volume alone. Support students with comparison and critique; extend through bias analysis, alternative prompts and evidence-based revision.
