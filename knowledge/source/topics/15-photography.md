## Photography as technical and visual practice

Photography records light to create images for documentation, expression or communication. Exposure, focus, viewpoint, timing, lighting and composition interact; technical correctness is judged in relation to intent.

## Exposure and focus

| Variable | Main effect | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Aperture | Light admission and depth-of-field behaviour | Focus range and lens performance |
| Shutter time | Recorded motion and exposure duration | Blur versus freezing movement |
| ISO setting | Image brightness interpretation/processing and noise behaviour | Quality and usable exposure choices |
| Focal length | Field of view with a given sensor | Framing and required camera distance |
| Focus distance | Plane of sharp focus | Subject separation and depth relationships |

ISO does not physically increase light captured. Exposure discussions should distinguish available light, sensor capture and processing. Depth of field also depends on distance, focal length, format and the criterion for acceptable sharpness.

Perspective depends on camera position. Wide-angle distortion at close distance is not simply caused by the lens label. White balance adjusts interpretation of colour illumination; mixed lighting may require local correction or changed lighting.

## Composition and lighting

Consider subject/context relationships, figure–ground, edges, negative space, balance, direction, viewpoint and moments. The rule of thirds is one organising option, not a guarantee of quality. A deliberate central composition can be appropriate.

Light direction defines form; apparent source size relative to subject affects softness. Reflectors, diffusers and flags modify illumination. Product photography may prioritise shape, detail and colour consistency; portraits may prioritise expression and relationship; documentary work may prioritise accurate context and ethical representation.

## Editing and image management

Preserve originals and use non-destructive adjustments where practical. Separate correction from interpretive treatment. Cropping changes meaning as well as composition. Sharpening cannot fully recover information that was never captured, and aggressive noise reduction may remove texture.

RAW preserves camera data for flexible processing but requires compatible interpretation. JPEG commonly uses lossy compression; PNG is useful for lossless graphics and transparency. Choose formats for the actual workflow. Pixel dimensions, compression and final output size together affect reproduction; a nominal ppi value alone does not add detail.

Batch adjustments can create consistency, but different lighting and skin tones may need individual corrections. Proof images under intended display or print conditions.

## Ethics and access

Plan permission, privacy, dignity, accurate context and disclosure of meaningful alterations. Do not treat online availability as reuse permission. Alternative text should communicate the image's purpose; complex or assessment-critical images may require longer descriptions or equivalent access.

## Teaching application

Use controlled comparisons: constant subject and viewpoint with varied shutter time; constant framing with varied camera position; constant lighting with alternative composition. Support with auto modes plus intentional framing before manual complexity. Extend through mixed-lighting trials, contact-sheet evaluation and documented editing choices.

**Capture/edit choice → observable effect → purpose → viewer/context → evaluation.**
