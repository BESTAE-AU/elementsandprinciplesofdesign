## Animation and motion graphics

Animation creates perceived movement through changing images or states. Motion graphics commonly animates graphic communication such as type, symbols and diagrams. Character animation, experimental animation and motion graphics overlap but have different aims.

Time is a design variable. Timing is the duration or placement of an action; spacing is the change in position between successive states. Identical duration with different spacing creates different acceleration.

## Core principles and techniques

| Concept | Function |
|---|---|
| Keyframe | Defines a property/state at a time |
| In-between/tween | Generates intermediate states |
| Frame-by-frame | Individually develops changing images |
| Anticipation | Prepares the viewer for an action |
| Staging | Makes the important action readable |
| Ease in/out | Changes acceleration near the start/end |
| Follow-through/overlap | Different parts continue or move at different times |
| Squash/stretch | Suggests elasticity or force while managing apparent volume |
| Arcs | Curved paths can support organic movement |
| Secondary action | Supports the primary action without competing |
| Exaggeration | Clarifies or heightens an intended effect |
| Appeal/solid drawing | Supports expressive clarity and believable structure where relevant |

The classic twelve animation principles are a historical character-animation framework, not compulsory rules for every diagram or interface. Straight motion and restrained timing may suit technical information.

## Motion communication

Animated hierarchy involves order of appearance, duration, scale, movement and contrast. A title that arrives too quickly may be unreadable even when its static typography is clear. Motion rhythm arises from repeated and varied timing. Temporal contrast can distinguish moments through stillness, pace or change.

Loops require a purposeful cycle and continuity at the seam. Motion paths, easing curves, rotation, scale and opacity should be selected for effect rather than randomly combined. Match sound and movement when synchronisation supports meaning.

## Production

Plan concept, storyboard, animatic, assets, layer structure, timing and export. An animatic is a timed sequence used to test pacing before expensive production. Choose vector or raster assets according to style and scaling. Transparency, frame rate, codec support and file size affect delivery.

Adobe Animate and After Effects serve different workflows; choose based on required production method and current supported formats. Do not assume that a project file is playable without its software or assets.

## Access and evaluation

Avoid essential meaning available only through rapid motion. Supply reduced-motion or static alternatives where relevant, usable controls and sufficient reading time. Check flashing against applicable accessibility guidance rather than relying on visual judgement alone. Captions and transcripts should describe relevant audio; diagrams may also need explanatory text.

## Teaching application

Have students animate one shape in contrasting ways while keeping duration constant, then explain spacing and perceived weight. Support with limited properties and a supplied timeline. Extend through graph-based easing, coordinated layers and alternative motion identities.

Assess intention, timing, clarity, consistency, technical finish and suitability. “More effects” is not equivalent to stronger motion design.
