## Portfolios as an evidence trail

A portfolio documents research, design development, production, testing and evaluation. Its purpose is to make learning and decisions traceable, not merely create a polished retrospective publication.

**Need → brief → research → criteria → alternatives → selection → tests → refinement → production → evaluation.**

## Core evidence

Include situation, identified need, purpose, audience, brief, criteria/specifications, relevant research and implications, initial ideas, developed concepts, annotated comparisons, trials, feedback, changes, production plans, time/cost records, quality checks, final evaluation and reflection.

Different projects require different evidence. Do not impose a fixed page count without the task. A failed sample with clear analysis may be valuable evidence.

## Documentation practices

Label versions, dates and purpose. Photograph meaningful stages with explanations. Separate what was planned from what occurred. Explain research implications and design changes. Cite sources and disclose permitted assistance according to the assessment.

Annotation should link features to effect and suitability. Evaluation should compare results with criteria. Reflection should identify learning and future practice.

## Ongoing portfolio auditing

An audit should be repeatable against the current task, rubric and actual files. Track stable student identifiers, submission versions, evidence locations, completion status, provisional criterion judgement, missing evidence and next steps.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Complete | Required evidence available and sufficient for the stated check |
| Partial | Some relevant evidence exists but a requirement remains unresolved |
| Missing | Evidence was not found in the accessible supplied material |
| Inaccessible | File/link could not be inspected |
| Not yet assessed | Available work has not received a judgement |

Do not treat inaccessible as missing or missing as proof the student did no work. Record scope and uncertainty.

## Dashboards and progress reports

Show dated progress, evidence gaps, priorities and changes since the previous review. Distinguish completion percentage from achievement quality. A mark must trace to rubric evidence; avoid arbitrary conversion of a checklist to a grade.

Individual student/parent documents can include completed requirements, remaining work, current provisional mark or level where justified, strengths and concrete improvement steps. The user's preferred level terminology is elementary, basic, sound, thorough and extensive.

Create reports in a clearly named project folder when requested and title files with verified student names. Preparing a report does not authorise emailing it; recipients and sending require the relevant explicit request and verification.

## Teaching and ethics

Teach evidence collection throughout the project. Support with a milestone map and sample annotations; extend through independent criteria, stronger comparisons and critical reflection. Protect student information and use approved systems for identified work.

AI auditing should quote or locate actual evidence, identify limitations and support teacher judgement. It must not infer motives, fabricate pages or claim an official final grade.
