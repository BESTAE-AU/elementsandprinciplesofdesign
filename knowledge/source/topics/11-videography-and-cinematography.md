## Planning video communication

Videography concerns capturing moving images; cinematography includes intentional visual choices about camera, light, lens, composition and movement. Define purpose, audience and viewing context before selecting techniques.

**Intended meaning → shot/lighting/sound decision → perceptual effect → audience response → suitability.**

## Shot language

| Dimension | Examples | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Shot size | Extreme wide, wide, full, medium, close-up, extreme close-up | Subject/context/detail relationship |
| Angle | Eye-level, high, low, overhead, tilted | Viewpoint and spatial presentation |
| Framing | Single, two-shot, over-the-shoulder, point-of-view | Relationships and viewpoint |
| Movement | Pan, tilt, dolly, truck, pedestal, tracking, crane, handheld, gimbal, slider | Changing view through time |
| Lens/focus | Focal length, focus pull, depth of field | Field of view and attention |

A pan rotates horizontally; a tilt rotates vertically. A dolly moves the camera towards or away; trucking moves laterally; pedestal movement changes camera height. Tracking describes following a subject and may use different equipment. A zoom changes focal length rather than camera position. Camera movement and zoom produce different spatial effects.

A focus pull changes the plane of focus to redirect attention. It is not camera movement. Perspective depends on viewpoint; changing focal length while keeping framing often changes camera distance and therefore perspective.

## Composition and coverage

Consider headroom, look room, foreground/background, figure–ground, leading lines, depth, visual balance and intentional off-centre framing. Conventions are tools, not universal rules.

Coverage supplies usable views of an action: establishing view, medium interaction, close detail, reactions and cutaways. Continuity involves screen direction, action, eyelines, props, lighting and sound. A storyboard visualises sequence; a shot list plans practical capture. Neither guarantees the final edit.

Multi-camera shooting captures an event from multiple views. Plan matching settings, synchronisation, sound, sightlines, continuity, storage and operator roles.

## Equipment selection under limited access

Organise decisions under camera, support/mounting, lighting, movement equipment, lens, power/storage, safety and audio. A phone, stable improvised support and controlled location may be sufficient if they meet the brief. Evaluate available equipment rather than require professional ownership.

**Equipment → capability → intended shot → project benefit → limitation → alternative.**

Lighting variables include direction, softness, contrast, colour and consistency. Capture intelligible sound close to the source. Test exposure, focus, motion, flicker and storage before filming.

## Teaching and evaluation

Ask students to communicate the same idea through two shot choices, then compare the effect. Support with a small shot bank and annotated storyboard. Extend through continuity challenges, multi-camera planning and controlled focus or movement trials.

Judge whether techniques contribute to communication, not only their count. In-camera monochrome or other effects may limit later flexibility; decide whether the effect should be captured or applied in post-production.

Exact shutter, frame-rate and export choices depend on lighting, intended motion, equipment and delivery. Verify equipment manuals and current software documentation rather than adopt one preset universally.
