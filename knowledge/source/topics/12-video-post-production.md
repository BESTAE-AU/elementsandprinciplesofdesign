## Editing as meaning-making

Post-production selects and organises captured material, creates relationships through sequence and duration, integrates graphics and sound, and prepares delivery. It is both technical and communicative.

**Brief → organise assets → select → assemble → refine picture → refine sound/graphics → export → inspect.**

An assembly gathers candidate material. A rough cut establishes structure. A fine cut refines timing and continuity. Picture lock is an agreed stable picture sequence, although classroom workflows may remain flexible. Document changes that affect sound or graphics.

## Editing techniques

Continuity editing supports coherent space and action. Montage creates meaning through juxtaposition or compression. Match cuts connect similar actions, shapes or ideas. Jump cuts create discontinuity, which may be purposeful. Cutaways conceal an edit or provide context. J-cuts introduce incoming sound before the picture; L-cuts continue outgoing sound into the next shot.

Pacing comes from shot duration, performance, movement, sound, information density and sequence. Faster cuts do not automatically create stronger engagement. A transition should establish a relationship or time change rather than merely demonstrate an effect.

## Technical workflow

| Area | Key decisions |
|---|---|
| Project organisation | Folder structure, names, versions, asset paths |
| Sequence | Frame size, aspect ratio, frame rate, working colour workflow |
| Proxies | Lighter editing media linked to originals |
| Picture | Trimming, synchronisation, reframing, continuity |
| Colour correction | Exposure, balance and matching |
| Colour grading | Intentional visual treatment after correction |
| Sound | Dialogue clarity, edits, music, effects, levels |
| Graphics | Hierarchy, legibility, timing, safe placement |
| Delivery | Codec, container, bitrate, resolution, captions |

Colour correction and grading have related but distinct aims. Proxies improve editing responsiveness; confirm that final delivery uses the intended source quality. Avoid repeated lossy recompression.

## Platform adaptation

Landscape and vertical versions often need different composition, graphics and pacing. Reframe shots deliberately; automatic centre crops may remove the subject or essential context. Verify current platform dimensions and length rules. Provide access to the message when sound is unavailable, while retaining meaningful audio for listening viewers.

Adobe's [export settings reference](https://helpx.adobe.com/uk/premiere/desktop/render-and-export/export-files/overview-of-export-settings.html) is the authority for version-specific controls, not a fixed classroom screenshot.

## Evaluation and teaching

Inspect the rendered file, not only the timeline. Check beginning/end, missing media, black frames, cuts, titles, captions, audible speech, synchronisation, dimensions and playback. Test on the viewing device.

Support with a supplied asset set and a short continuity task. Extend by producing two edits for contrasting audiences or comparing sound-led and picture-led sequences. Assess decisions with timecoded evidence.

Weak justification: “I used a dissolve because it looked good.” Stronger: “The dissolve links two related stages while implying elapsed time; a direct cut was tested but suggested immediate continuation.”
